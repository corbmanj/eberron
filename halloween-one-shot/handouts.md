# Handouts

Print or copy these for the players when they find them. The DM notes under each handout explain where it is and what it's for.

## Handout 1: The Church Notice

*Found in the study, Session 2. A printed notice, yellowed, pinned to the wall above the desk.*

> **WICKFORD CHAPEL OF THE HEARTH**
>
> **In Memory of Those Taken by the Grey Fever**
>
> *This winter past, the fever took from us:*
>
> Maisie Crane, aged 7 · Pip Hollis, aged 5 · Wren Albright, aged 9 · Tomas Albright, aged 6 · Bertram Lusk, lamplighter, aged 71 · Aveline Moss, midwife, aged 34 · Edda Vane, aged 8 · Colm Fairweather, aged 4 · Hester Pike, aged 11 · Jory Marsh, aged 12 · Nell Tanner, aged 6 · Benedict Ashe, baker, aged 45 · Lottie Greaves, aged 3 · Ansel Crow, aged 9 · Rosalind Marrowick, aged 8
>
> *and twenty-two others, whose names are kept in the chapel book.*
>
> *May the Hearth keep them warm.*

> **DM Note:** Tobias circled fourteen of the names in pencil. Rosalind's name isn't circled. Someone has underlined it twice, pressing hard enough to tear the paper. These fourteen, plus one more, match the dolls. The fifteenth doll is Rosalind's friend Dorrie Kemp, who died of the fever later in the spring, after this notice was printed. She's listed in the ledger.

## Handout 2: Tobias's Journal

*Found in the master bedroom, Session 2, on the nightstand. A thick leather book, the last third of the pages blank. Hand over these entries in order. Give the ritual page separately as Handout 3.*

> *Autumn.*
>
> Finished the Albright boy's jester today. Bells on every point of the hat. He shook it so hard one came off, and he laughed the whole time I fixed it. That is the whole of the job, really. To make them laugh.
>
> Still, I watch them play, and I see how soon they put the dolls down. A doll can't laugh back. A doll can't hold your hand. I have been trying for years to make one that feels like a friend. I haven't managed it yet.

> *Deepwinter.*
>
> The fever has come to Wickford. The chapel bell rings every day now. I closed the shop.
>
> Rosie is sick.

> *Deepwinter, later.*
>
> I buried my girl today. I made her a doll last month for her birthday, and she never got to hold it. I finished it anyway. It sits on her bed. I can't look at it and I can't put it away.

> *End of winter.*
>
> I have found it. I found it in one of my grandmother's old books, the ones she told me never to open. A soul does not leave the world all at once. For a little while after death, it lingers near what is left of the body. A little of that body, made into dust, can give it somewhere to stay.
>
> I thought of Rosie's doll. I sat by her grave all night with my tools in my coat. I could not do it. Not to her. I went home.

> *Early spring.*
>
> I did it. Gods forgive me, I did it, and it worked.
>
> Pip Hollis. Five years old. The fever took him three days ago. I took so little. So little. A single knuckle from his smallest finger, and no one will ever know.
>
> The eyes are the window the child looks through. I ground the bone in my mortar until it was finer than flour, and worked it into the paint. When I spoke the words, his doll opened its eyes and looked at me. Then it laughed.
>
> He's happy. He's playing on the workbench now. He doesn't remember being sick.

> *Spring.*
>
> Fourteen now. Fifteen with Dorrie Kemp, who only passed last week. I keep their names in the ledger in the painting room so I will never forget who they are.
>
> They are the finest dolls I have ever made. They play and sing all day. I've put a few in the shop window, and people stop to watch them. Soon I will make more, and every child in Wickford will have a friend who never leaves.

> *Summer.*
>
> Something is wrong. They don't sleep. They follow me from room to room. Edda cries at night, and when I ask her why, she can't say. Maisie keeps asking for her father.
>
> I tell myself they are happy. They were happy at first. Weren't they?

> *Autumn.*
>
> I've begun the tall one. A guardian, seven feet high, to walk the little ones home in the dark so no one is ever frightened on Ashen Lane again. I haven't carved his face yet. I want to get it right. For now he wears my old festival mask.
>
> His eyes are set aside in the drawer, waiting.

> *Hallows' Eve.*
>
> Aldous Crane was at the shop window tonight. He stood there a long time. Maisie was singing.
>
> He looked at me through the glass. I have never seen a man look like that.

> **DM Note:** The last entry is the final one. The party should come away knowing what Tobias did, why he did it, and that he believed he was doing good. The ritual page is tucked in the back of the journal.

## Handout 3: The Ritual Page

*Tucked in the back of the journal. A single page, written more carefully than the rest, with a diagram of a circle.*

> **To Give a Soul a Place to Stay**
>
> *It must be done while the soul is newly loosed. A soul lingers near its body only a short while, a day, no more, after death or after its remains are lifted from the earth.*
>
> **First.** Draw the circle on the floor around the vessel, in chalk or ash, exactly as shown. Seven rings. The marks between the rings must not touch.
>
> **Second.** Take one small bone from the remains. Grind it in the mortar until it is finer than flour. Work the dust into the vessel's eyes, and set the eyes in its face. The eyes are the window the soul looks through.
>
> **Third.** Speak the words over the vessel, and call the soul by its name:
>
> *"Here is a place to stay. Here is a window to look through. Come in out of the cold, [name]. Come in."*
>
> *The soul will come. Be patient with it. It will not know where it is at first.*

*[Diagram: a circle of seven rings. Between each pair of rings is a band of small hand-drawn marks: stars, keys, small hands, and closed eyes.]*

> **DM Note:** This is the party's instruction manual for Session 4. The phrase "after its remains are lifted from the earth" is the key line. It tells the players that digging up Tobias's bones makes his soul newly loose. Anyone who reads the whole journal, not just this page, gets advantage on the final ritual check.
