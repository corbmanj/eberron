# Session 1: Trick or Treat

## The Maker's House — Part 1 of 4

*A session for 4 characters of 8th level. Estimated runtime: 3 to 3.5 hours.*

---

## Session Overview

The party spends Hallows' Eve trick-or-treating in Wickford. At each door, the townsfolk drop pieces of an old story about a toymaker the town burned thirty years ago. All night, a tall figure in a white mask follows them. At the end of the night it stabs one of them, and nothing the party does can hurt it. It herds them to the end of Ashen Lane, where a kind old man waves them into a house that should be a ruin. The door locks behind them. The house is full of dolls, and the dolls are moving.

> **DM Note — Before You Start:** Read the Rules of the House in [README.md](../README.md). The Mark, the Sealed House, and the Bound Dolls all matter tonight. Decide in advance which character the Masked Stalker stabs. Pick a player who'll enjoy being the center of the scares, because that character becomes Marked for the rest of the adventure.

### Session Flow

| Beat | Type | Est. Time | Description |
|------|------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Roleplay | 15 min | Hallows' Eve in Wickford; costumes and introductions |
| 2 | Roleplay / Exploration | 50 min | Trick-or-treating: five doors, five pieces of the story |
| 3 | Combat | 25 min | The Masked Stalker strikes |
| 4 | Exploration | 10 min | Every street leads to Ashen Lane |
| 5 | Roleplay | 10 min | The Beckoning Man |
| 6 | Exploration | 15 min | Locked in |
| 7 | Exploration | 25 min | The dolls |
| 8 | Combat | 40 min | The Bound Dolls |

## Beat 1: Hallows' Eve in Wickford

Start with the players in the street. Before the read-aloud, go around the table. Each player introduces their character and describes their costume. Let them explain why they're in Wickford, using the hooks in the README if they need one.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> Wickford is a farming town of maybe three hundred people. Tonight, every house on the main road has a carved pumpkin on the front step, and every pumpkin has a candle inside it.
>
> Strings of paper lanterns hang across the street from roof to roof. They're orange and yellow, and they sway in the wind. Under them, the street is full of people in costumes. Kids in bedsheets with eyeholes cut out. A man dressed as a scarecrow, with straw sticking out of his sleeves. Two girls in paper crowns, holding hands.
>
> Somewhere down the road, someone is playing a fiddle. It's cold enough to see your breath. The air smells like woodsmoke, hot cider, and cut pumpkin.
>
> A boy in a paper bat mask runs past you with a sack of candy. He's shouting "Trick or treat!" at the top of his lungs.

Let the players settle in. The festival is cheerful and safe right now. The contrast is the point.

> **DM Tip:** Wickford's custom is that everyone in costume trick-or-treats, adults included. Kids get candy. Adults usually get a cup of cider or a sweet. Nobody will think twice about four adventurers knocking on doors.

## Beat 2: Trick-or-Treating

The party can visit the doors in any order. Door E should be last. Run the Stalker Sightings table between doors: one sighting each time the party moves to a new door.

Each door has a treat. Roll a d8 for each character who says "trick or treat." On a 7 or 8, they get an enchanted treat from the Treats of Wickford table below. Otherwise, they get ordinary candy.

### Stalker Sightings

Use one sighting each time the party walks between doors, in this order. The figure never moves while anyone is watching it. It's always farther away when they look directly at it, and never there when they go to check.

| # | Sighting |
|---|---|
| 1 | Across the street, under a lantern, a tall figure in a plain white mask stands still while the crowd moves around it. A hay wagon passes between you. When it's gone, so is the figure. |
| 2 | In a dark shop window, the party sees their own reflections, and behind them, the masked figure. When they turn around, the street behind them is empty. |
| 3 | In a group of costumed adults, one is a head taller than the rest, in a white mask and a long dark coat. It isn't moving with the group. When the group walks on, it's gone. |
| 4 | The Marked character, the one you've chosen, feels a light tap on the shoulder. Nobody is there. On the cobblestones behind them is a small pile of fresh wood shavings. |

### Door A: The Albright House

A bright, noisy house full of kids. Marta Albright, a cheerful woman in her forties, hands out candied apples from a basket. Her three children are in the doorway in homemade animal costumes.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The door opens before you can knock. A woman in a flour-dusted apron stands there with a basket of candied apples on her hip. Three kids crowd around her legs. One is dressed as a fox, one as an owl, and the smallest is wearing what looks like a bear made out of a brown blanket.
>
> "Trick or treat!" the bear shouts at you, very loudly.
>
> The woman laughs. "That's your line, sweetheart, not theirs. Here you go."

**What she knows:** Marta was a baby during the fever winter. It took her older brother and sister, Tomas and Wren. Her parents never talked about it much. She knows the old toymaker "did something to the graves" that year, and that the town "dealt with him." She doesn't like her kids going near Ashen Lane.

**What the kids know:** If the party asks, or if anyone mentions Ashen Lane, the kids giggle and start chanting the rhyme from [Handout 6](../handouts.md#handout-6-the-rhyme). Marta shushes them and pulls them inside.

### Door B: Granny Hettie Fairweather

A tiny house with a porch covered in pumpkins. Hettie is in her eighties, sharp and blunt. She sits in a rocking chair on the porch with a bowl of toffee on her lap.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> An old woman sits in a rocking chair on the porch, wrapped in three shawls. There's a bowl of dark toffee on her lap and a walking stick across her knees. She squints at your costumes for a long time.
>
> "Well," she says. "You're a big lot for trick-or-treating. Come here, then. Take two. They're good for the cold."

Hettie always gives an enchanted treat: Cinder Toffee, number 3 on the table below. She'll tell the story if asked, or if anyone mentions the masked figure.

**What she knows:**
- "Ashen Lane used to be called Mill Lane. They changed the name after."
- "Old Marrowick made toys. Good ones. Everyone had one. Then the fever came, and it took my grandson Colm. He was four."
- "Marrowick took the little ones twice. Once to the fever, and once to his shelves. That's what they said, anyway. They burned him on his own lawn for it."
- If the party mentions the masked figure, she stops rocking. "The tall one? Don't let him get close. He walks every Hallows' Eve. Folks who see him don't always come home."

### Door C: The Crane House

The only dark house on the street. There's no pumpkin on the step. One candle burns behind a thin curtain.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> This house has no pumpkin and no lantern. The porch boards are grey and warped. There's one light inside, a single candle behind a thin curtain in the front window.
>
> When you knock, the curtain moves. A long time passes. Then the lock turns, and the door opens about four inches on a chain. An old man looks out at you. He's thin and unshaven, and his eyes are red around the edges.
>
> "No candy," he says. "Go away."

This is Aldous Crane, in his seventies. He lit the fire thirty years ago. A **DC 15 Persuasion** check gets him talking, or any sincere mention of the masked figure or the toymaker. An Intimidation check makes him slam the door.

**What he knows:**
- "I had a daughter. Maisie. Seven years old. The fever took her in the winter."
- "That spring I walked past Marrowick's shop, and I heard her singing. Her song. Her voice. Coming out of a doll in his window."
- "We dug her up. Her little finger was gone. Fourteen more graves, the same. Something taken from every one."
- "I lit the fire myself. I'd do it again." He grips the door. "But every Hallows' Eve, I hear her. She's still singing. She's still in there."

He closes the door and won't open it again.

### Door D: Chapel Square

The town square, with the chapel on one side and a cider stand in the middle. Sister Orla, the chapel's keeper, is ladling hot cider for adults in costume.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The street opens into a small square with a stone chapel on the north side. A big iron pot of cider sits over a fire in the middle, and a line of adults in costume waits beside it with tin cups.
>
> The woman ladling the cider wears the plain grey robe of a chapel sister. She's about fifty, with her sleeves rolled up. She hands you each a steaming cup without asking.
>
> "No charge," she says. "It's Hallows' Eve."

The cider is hot and spiced, and it's safe. Sister Orla gives the official version of the story.

**What she knows:**
- The chapel records say the grey fever killed thirty-seven people that winter.
- In the spring, fifteen graves were found disturbed. A small bone was missing from each body.
- The chapel reburied the dead properly. The toymaker was "taken by the town" on Hallows' Eve, and his house burned.
- "The strange part is the dolls. People swore he had a shop full of them. When they searched the ashes, they never found a single one."

### Door E: The Ashen Lane Dare

Run this door last. On the corner where Ashen Lane meets the main road, a group of kids is daring each other to walk down the lane and touch the fence at the end.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> On the corner, half a dozen kids stand in a knot, pushing each other and laughing. They're looking down a narrow side street with no lanterns and no pumpkins. A signpost on the corner says **Ashen Lane**.
>
> At the far end of the lane, maybe two hundred feet away, is an iron fence. Behind the fence is what's left of a house. It's a black shell: two stories of burned timber, the roof caved in, the windows empty. In the front yard, a single wooden post stands up out of the dead grass. It's charred black.
>
> One of the kids is chanting. "One, two, the Maker's making you..." The others join in.

The kids finish the rhyme and dare each other to go touch the fence. Let the party watch for a moment. Then:

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> One girl takes a few steps down the lane, then stops.
>
> Beside the charred post in the yard, a tall figure is standing. White mask. Long dark coat. It wasn't there a second ago.
>
> The kids see it too. They scream and scatter in every direction.

The figure doesn't come down the lane. When the party looks back toward the main road, the crowd is thinning. People are heading home. Move straight to Beat 3.

### Treats of Wickford

When a character gets an enchanted treat, roll a d8. The treats don't spoil. A character can eat one as a bonus action.

| d8 | Treat | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Candied Apple Slice** | You regain 2d4 + 2 hit points. |
| 2 | **Owl-Eye Mint** | You gain Darkvision out to 60 feet for 1 hour. If you already have it, its range increases by 30 feet. |
| 3 | **Cinder Toffee** | You have Resistance to Fire damage for 1 hour. |
| 4 | **Whisper Wafer** | You can cast *Message* once within the next hour without spending a spell slot or components. |
| 5 | **Skeleton Crunch** | When you would gain the Frightened condition within the next 24 hours, you can choose not to gain it. The treat is then used up. |
| 6 | **Honey Moon Drop** | You have Advantage on the next Wisdom saving throw you make within 24 hours. |
| 7 | **Sour Wisp** | Your eyes glow pale green for 1 hour, shedding Dim Light in a 10-foot radius. |
| 8 | **Trick!** | A sugared lemon. Your tongue turns black and your voice squeaks for 1 hour. No other effect. |

> **DM Tip:** The Cinder Toffee is a quiet setup for the final fight, which is full of phantom fire. If a player saves theirs for three sessions, let it pay off.

## Beat 3: The Masked Stalker Strikes

The party is walking back along Lantern Row, the main festival street. The crowd is thinner now. Many lanterns have burned out.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The festival is winding down. Half the lanterns over Lantern Row have gone out. Parents are carrying sleeping kids home over their shoulders. The fiddle has stopped.
>
> A group of costumed adults walks toward you, laughing about something. As they pass, one of them doesn't keep walking.
>
> It's taller than the rest. It's wearing a plain white mask with rows of small holes drilled in it, and a long dark coat. In its right hand is a carving knife, long and thin, the kind a woodworker uses.
>
> It's already close enough to touch.

The Masked Stalker attacks the character you picked, with Advantage. Then roll initiative.

**The Masked Stalker.** See [encounter_street_masked_stalker.md](encounter_street_masked_stalker.md) for the stat block, tactics, and battle map.

> **DM Note:** This fight can't be won. The Stalker can't be hurt, and after its first stab it stops dealing damage. It pushes, blocks, and herds. The goal is for the players to realize it's invulnerable and run. Most tables figure this out in two or three rounds. If they keep fighting, have a townsperson scream "Run! It doesn't die! It never dies!" from a doorway.

### The Wound

When the Stalker's knife hits, describe it plainly. Don't mark it as special yet. The stitches come later, inside the house.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The knife goes in under your ribs and comes back out. It's fast and clean. There's a lot of blood, and it's warm through your costume.
>
> The masked figure doesn't pull back for another swing. It just stands there, looking at you through the holes in the mask.

## Beat 4: Every Street Leads to Ashen Lane

When the party runs, the Stalker doesn't chase them. It walks. It's always there when they stop.

- **Whichever way they run, they come out on Ashen Lane.** Side streets bend back. Alleys come out at the same corner. The road out of town ends at the Ashen Lane signpost. Describe it plainly the first time, and let the players notice the pattern themselves.
- **The Stalker is always behind them.** Each time they stop, it's standing at the far end of the street they just left, walking toward them.
- **The town is closed.** Doors are locked and lights go out as they pass. Nobody answers a knock.
- **The Marked character's wound keeps bleeding.** It doesn't close no matter what healing they use. Healing restores hit points normally, but the cut stays open.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> You come around the corner at a run, and you're back at the signpost. **Ashen Lane.** You didn't turn onto it. You came from the other direction.
>
> Down at the end of the lane, the iron fence. Behind it, the burned house.
>
> Behind you, at the far end of the street you just ran down, a tall figure in a white mask is walking toward you. It isn't hurrying.

## Beat 5: The Beckoning Man

When the party finally goes down Ashen Lane, or when they've run in circles long enough, the house changes.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The house at the end of Ashen Lane isn't burned anymore.
>
> It's a two-story wooden house, painted dark green, with white trim around the windows. Every window is lit with warm yellow lamplight. There's a carved pumpkin on each side of the front steps. There's a hanging sign over a side door that says **MARROWICK TOYS** in painted gold letters.
>
> The iron gate is open. An old man is standing in it. He's short and a little stooped, in a brown cardigan with patched elbows. He has round spectacles and a white beard, and he's holding up a lantern.
>
> He looks at the blood on your clothes. "Oh," he says. "Oh, you're hurt. Come in, come in. I have bandages inside."
>
> He turns and walks up the path. The front door is standing open. He goes inside.

This is the Beckoning Man, Tobias's memory of himself. He doesn't answer questions. If a character touches him or attacks him, their hand or weapon passes through him, and he keeps walking as if nothing happened.

> **DM Note — If They Won't Go In:** The Stalker keeps coming. It's at the top of Ashen Lane now, walking toward them. The Marked character's wound keeps bleeding. Every direction still leads back here. If the party still refuses after a few minutes, the lamps in the house flicker, and a child's voice calls from inside: "Please. Please come in." Most parties go in. If yours truly won't, let the Stalker walk up and stand one step away from them, and just wait.

## Beat 6: Locked In

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The foyer is warm. Oil lamps are lit on the walls. There's a staircase straight ahead of you and a tall grandfather clock beside it. The clock's hands point to one minute before midnight. It ticks.
>
> There's no sign of the old man. There's no lantern. There are no footprints on the floorboards except yours.
>
> Behind you, the front door slams shut. You hear the lock turn.

The door is now sealed. See The Sealed House in the README. Let the players try to get out. Show them it loops the first time, and don't let them waste too much of the session on it.

**Trying the door.** It doesn't budge. A **DC 20 Strength (Athletics)** check or a *Knock* spell opens it. Beyond it is the foyer, seen from the outside, and the characters step into it through the front door.

**Trying a window.** It opens easily. Anyone climbing through drops into the foyer through the front door.

### The Stitches

Once the party is inside, the Marked character's wound finally closes.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The wound under your ribs stops bleeding. When you look at it, the cut is closed. It's been sewn shut with a neat row of small black stitches. Nobody sewed it.

The stitches can't be cut or pulled out. They don't hurt. This character is now **Marked**. See The Mark in the README. From now on, when this character is near a doll, they hear faint whispers.

## Beat 7: The Dolls

Let the party explore the main floor. Use [house_main_floor.svg](../maps/house_main_floor.svg) and the room key in [maps.md](../maps/maps.md). Rooms 1 through 9 are available. The cellar door in the pantry is locked. The upstairs is available too, but most parties explore the main floor first. If they head upstairs, see Session 2 for the upper floor rooms.

The fifteen dolls start where the ledger in [Handout 4](../handouts.md#handout-4-the-ledger-of-the-dead) says. At first, the party only notices a few. More appear the longer they look around.

### The Dolls Move

Every time the party leaves a room and comes back, one doll in it has moved: turned its head toward the door, crossed its legs, moved to a different chair. Nobody ever sees one move.

Describe them the way the ledger describes them. Keep it simple and physical: the jester's bells, the ballerina's raised arms, the baker's rolling pin.

> **Read Aloud — The First Doll:**
>
> On the fourth step of the staircase sits a doll. It's a wooden boy about two feet tall, in a red soldier's coat with brass buttons. There's a small tin drum hanging at his hip on a string.
>
> His painted eyes are open. They're glass, and they catch the lamplight.

> **Read Aloud — The Toy Shop:**
>
> This room is a shop. There's a wooden counter with a brass bell on it, and long shelves along the walls. The shelves are mostly empty, except for a few dolls sitting in a row.
>
> A jester in red and gold, with bells on every point of his hat. A stable boy in a flat cap, sitting on a carved horse. A girl in a green coat. On the counter, a ballerina in a pink tutu, arms up over her head. On a low table there's a wicker basket, and in the basket is the smallest doll you've seen yet. It's cloth, with long rabbit ears.
>
> In the front window, facing the street, sits a rag doll in a blue pinafore. Her braids are made of yarn.
>
> All of them are facing the door you just came through.

**What the Marked character hears.** When the Marked character is within 10 feet of a doll, they hear a faint whisper. Use one per doll the first time:

- Pip, the soldier: "I'm not sick anymore. I'm not sick."
- Maisie, in the window: humming a song, very softly. If asked, it's a lullaby.
- Tomas, the jester: "Where's Wren? Is Wren here?"
- Lottie, in the basket: crying. Just crying.
- Bertram, by the clock: "It's nearly time. It's nearly midnight. I have to light the lamps."

**Investigating a doll without touching it.** A **DC 15 Wisdom (Perception)** check shows fine grey grit caked in the corners of the doll's glass eyes. It doesn't mean anything yet. It will after the journal.

### The Trigger

As soon as anyone touches, picks up, moves, or attacks a doll, all fifteen come to life at once. Don't warn the players. They'll find out.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The doll's head turns in your hand. Its glass eyes look up at you.
>
> Then, all through the house, you hear it: small feet on wooden floors. Dozens of them. Coming from every room at once.
>
> And somewhere, a child starts to laugh.

Roll initiative.

## Beat 8: Encounter: The Bound Dolls

**15 Bound Dolls.** They don't all arrive at once. See [encounter_toyshop_bound_dolls.md](encounter_toyshop_bound_dolls.md) for the stat block, the arrival schedule, tactics, and battle map. The encounter assumes the fight starts in the toy shop. If it starts elsewhere, use the same arrival rules from that room.

> **DM Note:** This fight should end with the dolls knocked down, not freed, unless someone works it out. When a doll drops near the Marked character, have it whisper its name, or "my eyes, please." A player who crushes a fallen doll's eyes sees a small light rise out of it. Don't explain what that means. Let them talk it over.

### After the Fight

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The last doll drops and doesn't move. The house is quiet again, except for the clock.
>
> They're lying all over the floor: on their faces, on their backs, arms bent the wrong way. Porcelain, cloth, and painted wood. The glass eyes are still open.
>
> Somewhere upstairs, a floorboard creaks.

Let the players react. Then close the session:

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> At the top of the staircase stands the old man in the cardigan. He's holding his lantern. He looks down at the dolls on the floor for a long moment.
>
> Then he looks at you. He turns and points down the upstairs hall.
>
> The lantern goes out. When your eyes adjust, he's gone.

**End of Session 1.**

> **DM Note — Session Wrap-Up:** Record which dolls were freed, if any, on the ledger in [handouts.md](../handouts.md). The party is on the main floor, probably wounded. They'll want a rest soon. A short rest is safe. A long rest pulls them into the first dream, which opens Session 2's second half. If they try to long rest immediately at the start of Session 2, let them explore upstairs first. Point out that the Beckoning Man wanted them to go up there.
