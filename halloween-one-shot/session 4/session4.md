# Session 4: The Faceless Doll

## The Maker's House — Part 4 of 4

*A session for 4 characters of 8th level. Estimated runtime: 3 hours.*

---

## Session Overview

The party performs Tobias Marrowick's own ritual on him. They draw the circle, grind his finger bone to dust, set the glass eyes in the faceless doll, and call him by name. The doll opens its eyes. Tobias wakes up inside it, believes it's still the night of the fire, and fights the "mob" with everything he has left. The party can destroy him, or they can make him understand he's already dead. Either way, the lock on the front door breaks. How the house ends depends on how he does.

> **DM Note — Session 3 Recap:** The party explored the basement and gathered the mortar, the chalk, and the glass eyes labeled "for the tall one." They dug up Tobias's bones from under the kiln room floor while the house fought back. They drove off the Burned Maker in the second dream. His soul is now loose. The ritual has to happen within a day of lifting the bones.

> **DM Note — Before You Start:** Count the freed dolls on the ledger in [handouts.md](../handouts.md). Tobias's hit point maximum in the final fight is **210 minus 10 for each freed soul.** Every doll that hasn't been freed fights at his side. Also check which pieces of evidence the party is carrying: the journal, the ledger, a mirror, a broken or freed doll, and Rosie's doll or her mold.

## Beat 1: Preparing the Workshop

The ritual happens in the workshop, around the faceless doll. Use [house_basement.svg](../maps/house_basement.svg).

Walk the players through what they have. They'll need:

| Needed | Where It Is |
|---|---|
| The ritual page | Tucked in the journal |
| Chalk or ash | Workbench drawer, or the kiln |
| A small bone from the remains | Tobias's bones, in the kiln room |
| The mortar and pestle | Workbench |
| The vessel | The faceless doll, standing in the workshop |
| Glass eyes | The pair labeled "for the tall one" |
| Tobias's name | The portrait nameplate, the journal, Aldous Crane, or *Speak with Dead* |

If they're missing anything, let them go get it. The house doesn't interfere yet.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The workshop is quiet. The lamps on the workbench burn low and steady. The faceless doll stands in the middle of the floor, facing the kiln room door.
>
> In the doorways, dolls are sitting. They weren't there a minute ago. They're just sitting, with their legs straight out in front of them, the way children sit on the floor. They're watching the faceless doll.

The unfreed dolls gather in the workshop doorways and watch. They don't attack during the ritual. Count them. These are the dolls that fight in Beat 3.

## Beat 2: The Ritual

The ritual has three steps, done in order. Each step needs one successful check. Any character can attempt any step. A failed check deals psychic damage to the character who made it, and they or someone else can try again. Each attempt takes about a minute.

| Step | What They Do | Check | On a Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Draw the circle from the ritual page around the faceless doll: seven rings, with the marks between them not touching | **DC 15** Intelligence (Arcana) or Intelligence (Religion) | 11 (2d10) Psychic damage |
| 2 | Grind the finger bone in the mortar until it's finer than flour. Work the dust into the glass eyes, and set the eyes in the doll's face | **DC 15** Dexterity check using Tinker's Tools, or Wisdom (Medicine) | 11 (2d10) Psychic damage |
| 3 | Speak the words over the vessel, and call him by name: "Come in out of the cold, Tobias Marrowick. Come in." | **DC 15** Intelligence (Religion) or a Charisma check | 16 (3d10) Psychic damage |

**Advantages:**
- **Step 2:** Using Tobias's own tinker's tools, found in the workshop, gives Advantage.
- **Step 3:** A character who read the whole journal, not just the ritual page, has Advantage.
- **Help:** Another character can take the Help action on any step, as normal.

### The House Resists

Each time a check fails, the house pushes back. Use one of these per failure, in order:

1. **The lamps gutter.** The lamps on the workbench dim to almost nothing, then slowly come back.
2. **The dolls stand up.** All of the watching dolls get to their feet at once. They stay in the doorways.
3. **The clock stops.** Upstairs, the ticking stops. The silence lasts until the next check.
4. **Smoke.** The workshop fills with the smell of burning wood. There's no smoke anyone can see.
5. **The rhyme.** Every doll in the doorways starts whispering the rhyme, together, very quietly.

### Describing the Steps

> **Read Aloud — Step 1 Succeeds:**
>
> The last ring closes. Seven chalk circles on the stone floor around the faceless doll, with rows of small marks between them: stars, keys, little hands, closed eyes. None of them touch.
>
> The chalk lines look brighter than they should in this light.

> **Read Aloud — Step 2 Succeeds:**
>
> The bone grinds down under the pestle, slowly, until there's nothing in the bowl but fine grey powder. You work it into the painted irises with your thumb, the way the journal says. Then you press the eyes into the empty sockets in the porcelain face.
>
> They click into place. Two glass eyes, pale brown, in a face with no nose and no mouth. They look straight ahead.

> **Read Aloud — Step 3 Succeeds:**
>
> You say his name.
>
> Nothing happens. Then all the dolls in the doorways turn their heads toward the faceless doll at the same time.
>
> The glass eyes blink.
>
> They look down at the chalk circle. Then at its own porcelain hands. Then up, at you.
>
> Somewhere upstairs, the grandfather clock strikes. Once.
>
> The doll's head tilts to one side. Porcelain grinds on porcelain. And from somewhere inside it, an old man's voice says, very quietly: "Oh, no. No, no. You're back. You came back with the torches."

Roll initiative.

## Beat 3: Tobias, the Faceless Doll

**Tobias, the Faceless Doll** and every unfreed Bound Doll. See [encounter_workshop_faceless_doll.md](encounter_workshop_faceless_doll.md) for the stat blocks, the Burning Memory lair actions, and the battle map.

He believes it's the night of the fire. He sees torches in the party's hands, whatever they're actually holding. The workshop fills with phantom fire and the shouts of a mob that isn't there.

### Talking Him Down

This option is open for the whole fight. The players don't have to choose between fighting and talking. They can do both.

- **The check.** A character within 30 feet of Tobias who he can hear takes an action to talk to him. They make a **DC 15** Charisma (Persuasion), Wisdom (Insight), or Intelligence (Religion) check. The player should say what their character says. Reward good ideas with Advantage even without evidence.
- **Evidence gives Advantage.** Showing him any of these gives Advantage on the check: the journal, the ledger, a mirror, a broken doll, or a freed doll.
- **Rosie's doll is special.** The first time a character shows him Rosie's doll, or her plaster mold, it counts as one success automatically. No roll is needed.
- **Each success makes him hesitate.** On his next turn, he makes one fewer Carving Glove attack. He can't take Legendary Actions until the start of that turn.
- **Three successes end the fight.** Go to Ending A below.
- **A failure has no extra cost.** The character just loses their action. If they fail by 5 or more, he lashes out at them with one Carving Glove attack as a free reaction.

**What he says as the successes build.** Use these to show the players it's working.

| Successes | He Says |
|---|---|
| 1 | "Dead? No. No, I have work to do. The children need me." |
| 2 | He looks down at his porcelain hands. "These aren't my hands. Why aren't these my hands?" |
| 3 | See Ending A. |

> **DM Tip:** When a player shows him a mirror, describe what he sees: not the porcelain face, but his own burned one. When a player shows him Rosie's doll, he stops completely. "That's Rosie's. I made that for Rosie." Give the moment room before the next turn.

## Beat 4: The Ending

### Ending A: He Understands

When the third talk-down success lands:

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> He stops. The carving glove drops to his side.
>
> "I'm dead," he says. It isn't a question. "They burned me. On the lawn. I remember."
>
> He looks at the dolls around the room. Some are still standing. Some are lying on the floor.
>
> "I only wanted them to be happy," he says. "Oh, what did I do. What did I do to them."
>
> Beside him, the old man in the cardigan is standing with his lantern. Neither of them looks surprised to see the other. The old man puts a hand on the doll's porcelain shoulder.
>
> "Let them go," the doll says to you. "Please. Their names are in the book. Let them go. And then let me go."

If any dolls are still bound, the party can free them now, by name or by their eyes. None of them resist. When the last one is free, or right away if they all already are:

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The glass eyes in the porcelain face close.
>
> The old man lifts his lantern. The flame in it goes out. When it does, he's gone, and the faceless doll is just a doll, standing in a chalk circle.
>
> Small lights rise out of the dolls around the room, one after another. They drift up through the ceiling and fade.
>
> Upstairs, you hear the front door lock click open.

**Leaving.** The house is calm. As the party climbs the stairs and walks through the foyer, the house changes around them. The lamps go out. The walls go black with old char. By the time they reach the front door, they're walking through the burned ruin, stepping over fallen beams. The door opens onto Ashen Lane. Go to the Epilogue.

### Ending B: He's Destroyed

When Tobias drops to 0 hit points:

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The porcelain cracks straight down the middle of the face, between the glass eyes. The crack spreads down the neck and across the chest.
>
> "Rosie," the voice inside says. "Rosie, go to your room. Stay in your room."
>
> The doll comes apart. It falls in large white pieces onto the chalk circle, and the glass eyes roll across the floor.
>
> Every doll in the room drops where it stands. Small lights rise out of all of them at once, and fade.
>
> Then you smell smoke. Real smoke. And upstairs, you hear the crackle of fire.

The fire Tobias remembered for thirty years finally finishes. The house is burning, and the party has to get out. The front door is unlocked now.

**Escape from the Burning House.** Run this as three rounds. Each round, every character makes the check for that stage. Freed souls help. If the party freed at least eight souls before the final fight, the lights of those souls drift ahead of them and show the way, and every character has Advantage on these checks.

| Round | Stage | Check | On a Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | The cellar stairs, full of smoke | **DC 13** Constitution saving throw | 7 (2d6) Fire damage, and 1 level of Exhaustion |
| 2 | The kitchen and back hall, ceiling coming down | **DC 14** Dexterity saving throw | 14 (4d6) Bludgeoning damage from falling beams |
| 3 | The foyer; the front door is blocked by a fallen beam | **DC 13** Strength (Athletics) check. Only one character needs to succeed to clear the door for everyone. | Each character takes 7 (2d6) Fire damage before the door is cleared on the next try |

A character who drops to 0 hit points can be carried out by another character, at half Speed. Cinder Toffee from Session 1 is very useful here.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> You come out through the front door into cold air and fall down the porch steps onto the dead grass.
>
> Behind you, the house is burning. Every window is full of orange light. The roof goes with a long, low crack, and a column of sparks goes up into the night.
>
> Then it's quiet. The fire is gone. The house behind you is a black shell, the way it looked from the street at the start of the night. Nothing in it is burning. The ash is cold.

Go to the Epilogue.

## Beat 5: Epilogue: Ashen Lane

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> You're standing in the front yard of the burned house on Ashen Lane. The iron gate is open. It's night.
>
> Down at the end of the lane, on the main road, the festival is still going. You can hear the fiddle. You can see paper lanterns, and kids in costumes running past with sacks of candy.
>
> It's the same night. By the look of the crowd, it's the same minute you walked through the gate.

The party was gone for days inside the house, and no time passed outside.

**The charred post.** It's still standing in the yard. In Ending A, it's cracked down the middle and leaning, the way old wood finally gives. In Ending B, it has collapsed into ash.

**Aldous Crane.** At the top of Ashen Lane stands the old man from Door C, in a coat thrown over his nightclothes. If Maisie's soul was freed:

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The old man from the dark house is standing at the top of the lane. He's in his nightshirt, with a coat thrown over it, and his feet are bare on the cold cobbles.
>
> "She stopped," he says, when you reach him. "Maisie. She stopped singing. For the first time in thirty years." He wipes his face with his sleeve. "Is she gone?"

If Maisie wasn't freed, Aldous is still at the top of the lane, but he says: "She's still singing. Why is she still singing?" Consider whether any of the unfreed dolls were left behind in the ruin. That's a hook for next Hallows' Eve.

### Closing Image

Choose one to end the adventure.

- **If every soul was freed:** A little girl in a paper bat mask runs past the party, laughing, and doesn't look back. For a moment, her laugh sounds like fifteen children laughing at once. Then it's just one.
- **If some souls weren't freed:** A little girl in a paper bat mask runs past the party. She's carrying a doll she didn't have when the night started. It's rag, in a blue pinafore, with yarn braids.

## Rewards

This is a one-shot, but the party earned something.

- **Tobias's Carving Glove.** See Loot in [encounter_workshop_faceless_doll.md](encounter_workshop_faceless_doll.md). It's in the ruin whichever way the fight ended.
- **Blessing of the Freed.** If the party freed all fifteen souls, each character gains a supernatural gift, a Charm with 3 uses. When you fail a saving throw against being Frightened, you can expend one use to succeed instead. You hear a child laugh when you do.
- **Wickford's gratitude.** Marta Albright, Granny Hettie, and Sister Orla make sure the party never pays for a meal or a bed in Wickford again. Aldous Crane gives them Maisie's old rag-doll ribbon, the only thing of hers he kept.

> **DM Note — If You're Continuing:** If you ever bring these characters back, Rosie is the thread to pull. Her soul was never bound, and she was never freed, because she was never trapped. Where did she go? Tobias sat by her grave all night. Maybe someone else didn't.
