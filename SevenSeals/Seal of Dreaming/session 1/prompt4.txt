Response: Perfect — introducing a *reluctant Kalashtar* is a fantastic way to weave your new player into the party and give her some built-in motivation. Since she’s new to D&D, giving her clear story beats, easy roleplay hooks, and a reason to rely on the party (without overwhelming mechanics) will help her ease in. Here’s a framework you can use:

---

## 🎭 The Reluctant Kalashtar (New Player’s Character)

**Name idea:** Sah’reth, Liraya, or Daelira (soft-sounding Kalashtar names that feel dreamlike)
**Role in the Story:** The *only way* into Dal Quor is through a Kalashtar willing to perform the Astral Projection ritual. But she has every reason *not* to go.

---

### ❓ Why She’s Reluctant

1. **The Quori are hunting her.**

   * Every Kalashtar is bonded to a rebel Quori spirit. Entering Dal Quor draws the attention of the Inspired (servants of the Dreaming Dark), who *will sense her presence*.
   * She knows going there paints a giant target on her back — and risks leading the party into deadly ambushes.

2. **She doesn’t trust outsiders.**

   * The Kalashtar live in secrecy (Adar, Riedra, or hidden enclaves). Humans, elves, etc., often view them as strange or dangerous.
   * The party needs her help, but she questions whether they’ll protect her, or abandon her once they get what they want.

3. **She has her own problem.**

   * A Quori agent (an Inspired assassin) has been tormenting her in her dreams and recently manifested in the waking world.
   * This agent is trying to kill her — and more importantly, sever her bond with her Quori spirit.
   * She doesn’t feel strong enough to face this alone.

---

### 🤝 What She Needs from the Party

1. **Protection from the Inspired**

   * She’s already been hunted and is on the move. She needs the party’s muscle to deal with assassins, spies, or a Dreaming Dark operative stalking her.

2. **Recovering a Lost Dream Anchor**

   * To perform the Astral Projection ritual, she needs an artifact known as a *Dream Anchor* (something infused with Quori essence, like a crystal shard, ancient idol, or a sliver of a giant’s dreamstone).
   * Hers was stolen or destroyed — the party must help retrieve/rebuild one.

3. **Facing Her Nightmare**

   * Her Quori spirit is unstable, whispering that her soul may not survive the journey.
   * To stabilize it, the party must help her undergo a **dream trial** (a shared dream sequence, or defeating the nightmare spirit that hounds her).
   * This could be a good early “tutorial” style session for the new player: she gets to see dream-logic storytelling, but the rest of the party is there to guide her.

---

### 🌌 Roleplay Hooks for the New Player

Keep it **simple, emotional, and easy to latch onto**:

* **Reserved & guarded:** She doesn’t like strangers knowing too much about her. If asked about her past, she changes the subject.
* **Dream-wary:** She has trouble sleeping, constantly checking her surroundings, afraid of nightmares intruding.
* **Reluctant ally:** She makes it clear she doesn’t want to go to Dal Quor, but admits it’s the only way to stop something worse.
* **Small sparks of hope:** If the party shows genuine kindness, she might warm up — hinting she hasn’t felt safe in a long time.

---

### 📖 Suggested Introduction Scene

* The party returns to Breland to meet Gendrick (in his human form).
* He introduces the Kalashtar, who looks visibly uneasy.
* She interrupts him:

  > “No. I’m not going. You don’t understand — if I step into Dal Quor, they’ll *see me*. They’ll hunt all of us. I won’t doom strangers for a fight that isn’t theirs.”
* Gendrick reassures her the party are no ordinary strangers — they’ve already faced demons, dragons, and worse. But she *only* agrees to join if the party helps her:

  1. Recover the stolen Dream Anchor (a short arc in Breland or Adar).
  2. Protect her from the Inspired assassin who is closing in.

This gives her an immediate personal hook, a reason to stick with the party, and an easy way to rely on them until she (and the new player) feel more comfortable.
