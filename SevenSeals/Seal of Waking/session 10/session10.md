## Seal of Madness — Act II: The Sacrifice

*A session for 4 level-10 characters. Estimated runtime: 3.5–4 hours.*

---

## Session Overview

The ritual is already in progress. The party descends into the lower forgeworks, confronts the woman running it, and arrives too late to stop what she started. The rift tears open anyway. On the other side is a version of Sharn that has never seen daylight, a dragon who has been working this problem for nine centuries, and the moment he understands what he actually did.

This is the last session before the campaign transitions to Vecna: Eve of Ruin. Play it accordingly — the party has carried six seals across the world and it ends here, in a dark copy of the city they live in, because someone else planned better.

---

They cleared the upper level, defeating the cult's Inquisitor and four cultists. The lower level is below them. The ritual light pulses through the grating.

## Beat 1: Descent

The stairs from the upper forgeworks are iron, narrow, and descend in a straight run — no turns, no landings. The sound builds as they go down. Not loud. A sustained pressure, like a chord held too long.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The stairs go down for longer than they should. The building above is four stories. The stairs feel like more.
>
> The light changes partway down: the gas lamps stop, and the only illumination comes from below — pale blue-white, cold, wrong. The walls are carved. Not the angular Severed Tongue glyphs from the upper level, but older marks, pressed into the stone in long vertical columns. They look like they were here before the forgeworks was built.
>
> At the bottom of the stairs, a door stands open.

The carvings are Dhakaan-era — this is a pre-Galifar structure that the Cannith forgeworks was built over and largely incorporated. The cult chose this location specifically because of what's underneath: a natural resonance point where the planar membranes are thin. Gendrick's network missed it.

**Before the door:** The party can hear the room now. Three sounds beneath the sustained tone — fragmented, rhythmic, like breathing that forgot what it was for. If they've met the three captives during the investigation, they can make a DC 14 Perception check to recognize any of the voices.

## Beat 2: The Ritual Chamber

### The Chamber

The lower chamber occupies the full footprint of the forgeworks. Unlike the industrial space above, this room was shaped deliberately — not built, but carved. The ceiling is domed. The walls curve inward. In the center, three circles are inscribed in the stone floor, burning with that pale blue-white light, each large enough to stand in.

In each circle: one of the three captives. Savia Patellus, upright but not quite present — her eyes open but tracking nothing. Bixum Worthwistle, smaller inside the circle than he looks in his council seat, his lips moving without sound. Umberto Gromwarden, the largest of the three, on his knees, a notebook open in his hands that is burning from the inside out, page by page, without flame.

Two cultists stand at the perimeter. A tall figure moves between the circles.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The room is cold. Your breath is visible.
>
> Three people stand inside rings of carved light — a woman in a lawyer's clothes, a gnome in council dress, a bugbear with a burning notebook. They are alive. They are not here.
>
> Between the circles moves a tall figure in dark robes. She writes in the air as she moves — a brush in one hand, leaving characters that hang for a moment and then dissolve. She does not look up when you enter. She finishes the character she's writing.
>
> Then she turns.
>
> Her face is calm. Her tongue, when she opens her mouth to show it to you, is carved with a glyph the size of a coin. She has been silent for a very long time and she is not afraid of you.

### After the Fight

Soreth is unconscious or dead. The cultists are down. The ritual circles are still active — their light unchanged. The captives are still inside them.

The party can free the captives. Stepping into a circle disrupts its containment — this is safe for the party, and the captive inside is released. Each captive wakes disoriented but physically intact. Their memories are present but blurry at the edges — they know who they are, but certain things feel like trying to read a page that is slightly out of focus.

> **DM Note:** The three captives are alive, cogent, and deeply grateful. If the party wants a brief interaction before the rift opens, allow it — but keep it short. Savia asks what day it is. Bixum looks at his hands. Umberto immediately checks his notebook: the pages are charred to the edges but the writing on them is intact — the extraction failed before it was complete. He says, quietly: *"Something stopped it."* He means the party.

**The rift opens anyway.**

Even though Soreth is down, the ritual progressed far enough. Vecna does not need her to complete it.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The circles go dark. All three, at once.
>
> Then the center of the room tears.
>
> Not an explosion — it opens like something pulling at a seam, the stone floor splitting along a line that wasn't there a moment ago. The light that comes through is not the pale blue-white from before. It is the absence of light, with just enough to see by. A city on the other side of the split, looking back at you.
>
> Sharn. But the towers are wrong — shorter, darker, the cloud layer where it should be but the light coming from below instead of above. The bridges are there. The districts are where they should be. Everything is in the right place and nothing is alive.
>
> The rift pulls. You don't jump. You just go through.

The captives do not go through. They are left in the lower forgeworks, shaken but present. Someone will find them.

The party has no choice in the crossing. The rift pulls them through, closes behind them, and they are in the Hollow.

## Beat 3: The Hollow

This is Sharn in Dolurrh's shadow — not the plane of the dead itself, but the impression Sharn has left on the membrane between worlds. Every city of sufficient age and weight leaves an impression. Sharn's is enormous. Vecna found it and used it as a staging ground.

The sky above is not sky — it is the color of deep water, still. The towers around them are present but dim, as if seen through smoked glass. The streets have the proportions of Sharn but no people, no movement, no sound beyond the party themselves.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The city looks like Sharn if you don't look too long at any one thing.
>
> The towers are the right height in the right places. The bridges connect what they should connect. The street beneath your feet has the same uneven paving of the mid-district you've crossed a hundred times. But the light comes from somewhere below the city, not above it, and the sky has the texture of stone. There is no wind. There are no birds.
>
> There is no sound anywhere except the sound you are making.

The Hollow is not hostile in any combat sense. There are no encounters here. This is the space between things — a place to stand in while something else changes.

**Navigation:** The party is in the Cogs equivalent of the Hollow. Gendrick is in the upper city equivalent — they can find him by following the towers upward, which requires crossing bridges that feel slightly wrong underfoot (solid, not transparent, but with the sense that they are maintained by expectation rather than stone).

> **DM Note:** The journey through the Hollow is meant to be atmospheric, not mechanical. If the party splits up or tries to explore, bring them to Gendrick within 15–20 minutes of real table time. You don't need a map. The Hollow bends toward where they need to be — they may notice this if someone makes a DC 13 Perception check. The city is oriented toward the same point.

**Signs of Vecna's presence:**

As they move through the Hollow, they find evidence of what this place has been used for:

- Ritual circles scratched into the Hollow's version of Sharn streets — dozens of them, most spent.
- In what would be the Market district: three empty chairs arranged in a triangle, an inkwell between them, a single sheet of paper face-down. If turned over, the paper has three names on it. The party's three captives. And below them, space for more names — a dozen blank lines.
- At the corner of what would be the Warden Bridge: a Sharn Watch uniform hanging from a post. Empty. Inside the collar, in a handwriting the party has not seen before: *Knowing is the only thing worth taking.*

## Beat 4: Gendrick's Last

Gendrick is in the Hollow version of his tower. Not inside it — the door is open, and he is sitting on the front step. He is in his human form. He has been here for some time.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The tower is where it should be, on the right street, same height, same worn stone around the door frame. The door is open.
>
> He is sitting on the step with his forearms on his knees, looking at the ground. He doesn't look up right away. When he does, there's something different in his face from every other time you've spoken to him — not surprise, not fear.
>
> He looks tired in a way that doesn't have anything to do with sleep.
>
> "I thought it might be you," he says. "I hoped it would be."

---

### The Conversation

Gendrick has had time to think. He understands more now than he did in the material plane — the Hollow clarifies certain things, the way silence clarifies things. He asks the party one question before he says anything else:

> **Gendrick:** "How many seals did I take from you? Over the years. How many times did I say *bring me the next one, we're close, this is the right thing to do?"*

He's not looking for absolution. He's establishing what he knows.

He has assembled the following in the hours or days he's been here:

- **He was watched from the beginning.** Not by Vecna directly. By something that worked through Vecna, through the Lords of Dust, through every villain the party has faced. Every seal retrieval served a plan he didn't write.

- **The seals are not what he thought they were.** He tells the party: *"They're a mechanism. A key made from pieces. I thought we were locking a door. We were building the key to open one."*

- **He knows what Vecna is.** Not who — what. He says it carefully:

> **Gendrick:** "Every face you've seen on this. Chataschesma. The Daelkyr. The figure Ozzy has been hearing through the crown. They are not different enemies. They are the same attention, wearing different expressions. I've spent nine centuries thinking about the problem and I missed this." A pause. "That is not a small thing to miss."

He does not name Nyarlathotep. He does not have that name. He describes the thing accurately without being able to call it anything. It is the party's knowledge to carry.

---

### The Realization

At some point — let the party lead the conversation where it goes, but bring it here — Gendrick reaches the thing he has been building toward:

> **Gendrick:** "The seals are here. In the Hollow. All six." He looks at his hands. Not a dragon's hands. Human hands, old, careful. "Vecna is going to take them. Not because I'll hand them to him — I won't. But because I've kept them together and this place belongs to him and there is no path where he doesn't get them."
>
> Another pause, longer.
>
> "Except one."

He describes what he can do. He has enough power left — the accumulated weight of nine centuries of careful work — to scatter the seals. Not destroy them. They can't be destroyed. But he can push them through the fraying planar membranes at the edges of the Hollow, send them across the multiverse to places that aren't here, places that Vecna would have to find one by one.

> **Gendrick:** "He'll find them. Eventually. He's patient. But it buys time, and time has always been the only thing that mattered in this." He looks at the party. "You'll have to find them again. I'm sorry about that. I know what I'm asking."

He is not asking. He is telling them what he is going to do.

> **DM Note:** The party can try to argue with him. Let them. He has an answer for every alternative — he's had time to think about it. The answer is always the same: the seals cannot stay together in a place Vecna controls. Anything else leaves Vecna with the mechanism. The only move that buys time is this one. If a player character wants to offer themselves as a vessel for one of the seals, or suggests Gendrick fight his way out, or offers a plan — engage with it genuinely. And then Gendrick declines it, gently, because he is nine hundred and seventeen years old and he has already thought of it.

---

### The Sacrifice

Gendrick does not make it dramatic. He stands up from the step. He straightens his robe. He looks at each of the party in turn.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> He puts one hand flat against the doorframe of his tower — his hand in this place, not a dragon's claw, just a hand. The stone of it is cold and dim and slightly wrong, the way everything here is slightly wrong.
>
> He says: "I spent nine centuries looking for a way to lock the door. I found a way to lose the key instead. That will have to be enough."
>
> He closes his eyes.

The seals leave his possession and scatter. The party cannot see where they go. The Hollow shakes — not like an earthquake, but like a sentence ending. The towers around them flicker. The false Sharn begins to lose the shape it was holding.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The light changes. The too-still sky above cracks along a line that runs from one horizon to the other. Through the crack: not daylight, but something with the idea of daylight in it.
>
> He is still standing at the door.
>
> Then he is not.
>
> The Hollow keeps the shape of his tower for a moment longer than it keeps anything else. Then that too goes.

---

### Vecna's Link

Before the Hollow collapses entirely, something finds them. Not a figure. Not a voice in any conventional sense. A temperature — the specific cold of being looked at by something that does not blink.

Each party member feels it at the same moment. And then, on the inside of their left wrist, a mark appears. No larger than a thumbprint. The color of a bruise that has not finished forming.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> It doesn't hurt. That's the wrong word for it.
>
> It feels like knowing, for just a moment, that you are inside someone else's plan. Not a new plan — an old one, one that has been running longer than you have been alive. You are inside it. You have been inside it for some time.
>
> The mark on your wrist is cold and it does not fade.

> **DM Note — Vecna's Link:** The mark is permanent and magical, resistant to removal (Dispel Magic does not remove it; Remove Curse at 6th level suppresses it for 24 hours before it returns). It has two effects:
>
> 1. **Presence.** Vecna is aware of where each bearer is, to within a mile. He is not watching them constantly — he has larger concerns — but he knows they exist and he knows their general location. Occasionally they will feel the mark pulse with cold, which means something nearby is relevant to his attention.
>
> 2. **Pull.** The bearers have faint awareness of the direction of Vecna's primary location. This is a narrative tool, not a GPS — it manifests as a vague sense of wrongness in a particular direction, a slight feeling that something important is somewhere specific. This will become relevant as Vecna: Eve of Ruin begins.
>
> The party may find the mark alarming. They are right to. They should not spend this session trying to remove it — they cannot, not yet. Tell them that if they try.

---

### Return

The Hollow collapses around them. The transition back to the material plane is not the wrenching pull of the initial crossing — it is slow, like a tide going out.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The shadow city fades in pieces. The street beneath your feet becomes the stone floor of the forgeworks. The dim towers become the walls of the ritual chamber. The too-still sky becomes the carved ceiling above.
>
> You are back in the lower chamber. The ritual circles are cold and dark. The captives are gone — someone came for them, or they walked out themselves.
>
> The grating above is faintly lit by the gas lamps of the upper level. The stairs are where you left them.
>
> Everything is as it was, except the marks on your wrists, and the weight of what you're now carrying.

>
> The "Mordenkainen" in Ozzy's crown is Kas. He does not know what the party knows. He has been trying to help and he has been wrong about almost everything. He is still a resource — and still, technically, the party's best lead on where to start looking for the scattered seals.
>
> Give the party a moment at the end of this session to process. Don't rush to the next hook. The weight of what Gendrick did deserves a breath.
