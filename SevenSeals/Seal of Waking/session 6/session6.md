# Session 6: The Hour That Holds

## Seal of Waking — Act VI: The Clock Tower

*A session for 4 level-9 characters. Estimated runtime: 3.5–4 hours.*

---

## Session Overview

The party climbs the corrupted tower in Irian from ground floor to the top — eight stories of worsening darkness, cult markings, and hostile creatures. At the summit they find the engine that holds Irian's eternal dawn in place: a massive clockwork mechanism, currently stopped at the wrong position. The Seal of Waking is locked inside it. To retrieve it, they have to briefly restart the clock, correct its position, and stop it again. The instant the clock runs, something notices.

> **DM Note — Session 5 Recap:** The party killed three Broken Dawnwardens outside the tower, which cleared the path and restored Irian's passive HP regeneration. They are standing at the ground floor arch. The party decided to rest here before climbing. The tower is eight stories tall. The lower floors are intact; the upper floors are dark and corrupted. Session 6 begins with the short rest.

## Beat 1: The Resting Place

The party is in the ground floor of the tower. The arch behind them opens onto the path back to Shae Mordai. The staircase winds up along the outer wall. Short rest here.

The positive energy of Irian applies during the rest. Remind the party of the 1 HP per turn rule — in the time it takes to short rest (1 hour), that's significant passive recovery.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The ground floor is a single circular room, maybe thirty feet across. The floor is the same pale stone as everything else in Irian — glyphs carved into every surface, dense and continuous, running up the walls in columns. Down here, they're still lit. Faint and warm.
>
> The ceiling above you is about twelve feet high — but above it you can hear the building. A deep silence, the kind a place has when it's very large and very still. Whatever is up there isn't moving.
>
> The staircase starts here. Stone steps, wide enough for two people, curving up and out of sight along the outer wall. The first few are the same pale stone as the floor. Three steps up, they start to darken.

---

## Beat 2: Floors 1–2

The staircase winds in and out of the building — some sections of it are exterior, open to Irian's dawn light, others pass through the tower interior. This creates a rhythm of light and dark as they climb.

Floor 1 is mostly intact. The glyphs continue up the walls. The vines haven't reached here yet, though the stone is cooler than it should be.

Floor 2: the vines appear. Thin at first — dark threads growing out of cracks in the mortar, reaching across the stone. They're clearly the same material as the tendrils that have been following the party since the vault in Shae Mordai.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The second floor landing has windows that face east — open-air, no glass, the dawn light coming through. You can see the path back to the manifest zone threshold from here. The garden. The way out.
>
> The wall beside the window has vines on it. Not plant vines — no leaves, no color. Black threads growing out of the stone itself, reaching across the surface. They don't move. They look like they've been there for months.
>
> Above you, the staircase curves out of sight. The walls up there are darker.

---

## Beat 3: Floor 3 — The Cult Carvings

This is the session's first major story beat.

The floor 3 landing is the most intact of the middle floors — a wider room with exposed exterior wall on three sides. The dawn light comes in strong here, and the vines are still thin. But carved into the stone, overlaid on top of the tower's original Aerenal glyphs, are markings the party has seen before.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The third floor landing is wider than the ones below it — the staircase opens into a room here. Three windows, floor to ceiling, looking out at Irian from above. The light is strong up here. That makes the carvings easier to see.
>
> Someone else has been in this room. The original glyphs on the walls — the dense, continuous columns of Aerenal script running from floor to ceiling — have been carved over. Different symbols. Deeper cuts. The work is rough and fast, not the careful craft of whoever built this tower.
>
> You've seen these symbols before.

Let the party react. They'll recognize the markings as the same cult symbols from the crypt in Shae Mordai.

**Investigation DC 13 or Arcana DC 13** to understand what the carvings are doing (as opposed to simply recognizing them):

*Success:* The symbols are the same type as what was in the vault — they're designed to weaken the connection between a protected space and its source of power. In the vault, they were weakening the basin. Here, they're targeting the mechanism at the top of the tower. They aren't trying to break the tower — they're trying to get it to *fail slowly.*

*Failure:* They're cult markings, clearly. They look recent. Beyond that it's hard to say without more context.

**Arcana DC 16** (can be attempted separately, with or without the first check):

*Success:* These are not complete. The pattern of symbols is unfinished — the cult started the working but didn't finish it. Whatever they were trying to do here, they stopped.

> **DM Note:** The cult was here within the last several months. They carved what they could on floor 3, made more progress on floor 7 (see Beat 6), and then left. Their notes on floor 7 explain why. The corruption to the tower is real — the cult's partial work has been degrading the mechanism at the top over time. But they couldn't finish the job without knowing the mechanism's correct position, and they couldn't find that. So they left, expecting the slow corruption to weaken the tower enough that they could return and finish.
>
> The party does not need to know all of this right now. Let them sit with the recognition.

## Beat 4: Floor 4 — Encounter: Void Bats

The fourth floor is where the vine coverage becomes heavy. The walls here are almost entirely black — the original stone barely visible beneath the tendril growth. The staircase on this floor passes through a landing with windows, but you wouldn't know it.

When the party rounds the curve of the staircase onto the floor 4 landing, the bats drop from the ceiling.

**6 Void Bats.** Roll initiative. See [encounter_floor4_void_bats.md](encounter_floor4_void_bats.md) for full stat block, tactical reference, and battle map.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> You come out onto the fourth floor landing. The windows here have been completely blocked — the tendrils have grown up the outside of the building and sealed them over entirely. The glyph-light from the lower floors doesn't reach here.
>
> Then something moves in the vines above you. Several somethings.
>
> They drop.

---

### After the Bats

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The last one comes apart the same way the others did — the shape losing cohesion, the void-matter going slack, then gone. No blood. No remains.
>
> The vines on the walls haven't moved. They're thicker here than below.

Positive energy regeneration (1 HP/turn) resumes between floors. Irian's healing ticks back in once the last bat is down.

## Beat 5: Floors 5–6 — The Scholar's Trail

### Floor 5: The Body

The fifth floor landing is a wider space — looks like it was once used for storage or study. Stone shelves are built into the walls. Most are empty. On the floor near the north-side window is a body.

It's an Aerenal elf — not Deathless, just dead. No wounds. The vines have begun to grow over her. She's been here for weeks. A leather satchel is beside her.

**Perception DC 11** to notice the satchel is partially tucked under her.

The satchel contains:
- A journal, partially filled
- A small kit of measuring tools (calipers, a folded ruler, a compass)
- A dried piece of fruit — the last thing she was eating

> **DM Note:** Her name, if the party checks her for identification: Ithyrael Vonaen, a historical scholar from Shae Mordai. She came here to study the tower. The corruption got her. The cause of death is exposure — the void-matter toxicity of the upper floors is slow but certain for anyone who stays too long without protection.

**The Journal** — the relevant pages:

The early entries are architectural notes, mostly dry. But about halfway through:

> *"The tower is not a monument. I was wrong about that. The lower glyphs are commemorative — the upper ones are functional. The mechanism at the summit is the purpose of the building. This structure exists to keep something stopped."*
>
> *"The mechanism is a clock that does not run. The pendulum is motionless. That is its correct state. The clock is designed to hold a specific moment — the first breath of dawn — in permanent stasis. The Seal is embedded within it, somewhere in the gear housing. I believe the Seal and the mechanism are interdependent. Neither functions without the other."*
>
> *"Someone has been here. The mechanism has been moved from its correct position — I can tell from the marker on the dial. It's been turned roughly thirty degrees clockwise from where it should be. Not enough to start running. Enough to make the Seal inaccessible. Clever."*
>
> *"I've sketched the dial on the facing page. The first-light mark is here — the small glyph that looks like a horizon line bisected by a ray. Whatever position the clock is in now, they've moved it away from that mark. To retrieve the Seal, someone would need to restart the mechanism, rotate back to first-light, and stop it there. I don't have the expertise to do that safely. I need to go back and consult Varaen."*
>
> *"I don't feel well."*
>
> *"I don't feel well. I'm going to rest."*

The facing page has a sketch of the clock dial — a circular diagram with symbols marked around it. The first-light symbol is circled in ink: a horizon line with a single ray rising from its center.

> **DM Note:** This journal is the party's primary mechanical guide for the puzzle. It tells them: (1) the mechanism needs to be briefly started, (2) the dial needs to be set to the first-light position, (3) then stopped. It does not tell them the order of the four steps — they'll have to work that out from the chamber itself and the clues they find on floor 7.

### Floor 6: The Upper Dark

Floor 6 is nearly completely overgrown. The vines cover every wall, ceiling, and most of the floor. Moving through is not difficult — the growth is dense but passive — but it's dark and close.

Through an exterior section of staircase between floors 5 and 6, the party can see the outside of the mechanism housing at the top of the tower — the original white stone of the tower gives way to a wider, domed section, and through gaps they can see the edges of enormous gear teeth.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The staircase opens to the outside here — a short exterior section before it turns back in. The air is cold this high up. The dawn light is coming from below you as much as ahead.
>
> Above, the tower gets wider. Not taller — wider. The roof of it isn't flat; it's domed, and bigger than the floors below it. Through the gaps in the stonework you can see something inside. Metal. Enormous teeth of metal, curved, like the edge of a wheel fifty feet across. The gears at the top of this tower are bigger than any gear has a right to be.
>
> The staircase goes up.

## Beat 6: Floor 7 — Encounter: The Tendril Warden + Cult's Final Notes

Floor 7 is where the cult spent most of their time. The carvings here are denser than floor 3 — more complete, more methodical. They cover the floor as well as the walls. Beneath the cult markings, scratched into the stone in Common, is a message.

The encounter happens immediately upon reaching the landing.

### The Encounter

**1 Tendril Warden.** It was stationed on this floor — the heaviest corruption point in the building. When the party arrives, it's motionless against the far wall, arms hanging, but it turns as they reach the landing.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The seventh floor landing is the biggest interior space in the tower. High ceiling. Three full walls of cult markings — not just carved over the Aerenal script this time, covering it entirely. The original glyphs are gone under the weight of it.
>
> In the center of the room, something large.
>
> It's made of the same void-matter as the bats, but not the same shape. This one is tall — eight feet at least — built roughly in the shape of the tower's construct-guardians. The shape of something that used to protect this place. The tendril material covers it completely. Where there should be a face, there's just mass.
>
> It turns toward you.

**Roll initiative.** See [encounter_floor7_tendril_warden.md](encounter_floor7_tendril_warden.md) for full stat block, tactical reference, and battle map.

---

### After the Fight: The Cult's Notes

Once the Warden is down, the party has time to look at the carvings on the walls. Beneath the cult markings, in Common, scratched into the stone:

> *"The mechanism resists. The anchor holds. We have tried to reach the position dial, but we cannot operate the clock face without first slowing the main drive. We tried to set the dial without braking the gear and the light shifted — badly. We stopped immediately."*
>
> *"We cannot find the correct position. The first-light mark is unclear. Without knowing where to stop the clock, we risk setting it running permanently. We are not prepared for that."*
>
> *"The tower will weaken with time. The corruption we have seeded should be sufficient to make it workable within a season. We leave. We return when it is ready."*

> **DM Note:** This is the party's second major clue for the puzzle. It tells them two things: (1) the Gear Brake must come before the Clock Dial — doing it in the wrong order shifts the light badly; (2) the cult couldn't find the first-light mark, which means the party's advantage (Ithyrael's journal) is exactly the edge the cult didn't have.
>
> The party should feel, reading this, that the cult is not beaten — they're delayed. They're coming back for this. And they were much closer to succeeding than anyone knew.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The carvings cover everything in here. But under them — scratched in Common, barely visible under the ritual marks — someone wrote something. The handwriting is fast and practical. Not the careful script of a scholar.
>
> They were here. They worked here. And they left on purpose.

## Beat 7: Floor 8 — The Clock Chamber

### Entering the Chamber

The top of the staircase opens through a heavy stone arch into the clock chamber. The arch has an inscription above it — old Aerenal, carved into the keystone. **History DC 11 or Arcana DC 11** to read it:

*"The breath before first light cannot be forced — release what holds it, slow what drives it, name the hour, then hold the moment."*

This is the sequence. Write it out for the players if they successfully translate it.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The chamber is enormous — sixty feet across, maybe fifty feet tall. The entire interior of the dome is mechanism.
>
> Gears fill the space from floor to ceiling: some the size of carriage wheels, some larger than the chamber is wide, their teeth interlocked and motionless. Chains hang between the upper assemblies. A massive pendulum arm extends from the central housing down toward the floor — it is not swinging. The whole apparatus is stopped.
>
> The floor is a narrow ring around the edge. From it, a metal walkway extends to a raised platform at the center of the room, where the gear housing is thickest. The Seal is in there — you can see it through the stopped gear teeth, a faint warm glow deep inside the mechanism. You cannot reach it. The gears are in the way.
>
> The light in here is wrong. Through four windows cut into the dome, you can see Irian's dawn outside. But inside the chamber, the light is dimmer. Slightly the wrong color. Whatever the corruption has done to this place, it's worse in here than anywhere below.
>
> The inscription above the arch is in old Aerenal. Four lines. Four instructions.

---

### The Mechanism: Elements and Layout

**The Turning Bridge**

The central walkway can be rotated. A release lever is mounted at the north end of the bridge (closest to the entrance arch). The bridge pivots on a central axis, swinging to face one of four anchor positions around the chamber wall: N (where it starts), E, S, W.

Each position gives access to a different mechanism mounted on the chamber wall. The party will need to rotate the bridge to reach the other three puzzle elements — the Escapement Release is on the north wall, accessible immediately.

*Hazard:* The first time someone pulls the release lever without warning the rest of the party, the bridge swings with a lurch. Everyone on the bridge: **DC 14 DEX save.** Fail: 2d6 bludgeoning damage, knocked prone (and holding on — they don't fall). After the first time, the party learns to brace before rotating.

---

### The Four Steps

**Step 1 — Escapement Release** *(North wall — accessible from the starting bridge position)*

A heavy iron lever mounted in a stone housing. Currently locked in the upright position. The housing has a single line carved into it: *"Release."*

Pulling the lever disengages the mechanism from its current (corrupted) stopped state. The pendulum begins to swing — slowly at first, then building. The gears begin to turn with a deep, structural groan. Through the windows, the light in the chamber shifts: the wrong-color flicker becomes more pronounced as the mechanism starts running.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The lever takes both hands. When it moves, something deep in the mechanism unlocks with a sound like a bell struck wrong — resonant, off-key. Then the gears begin to move.
>
> Slowly. Then less slowly.
>
> The pendulum arm swings. Once, then back, then forward again, each pass a little wider. Through the windows, the dawn outside doesn't change. Inside the chamber, the light flickers. It goes warm, then uncertain, then wrong.
>
> The clock is running.

---

**Step 2 — Gear Brake** *(East wall — requires rotating bridge to E)*

A large brake assembly connected to the main drive gear. Two handles: one marked with a symbol meaning *slow* (a horizontal line with decreasing height marks), one marked with a symbol meaning *reverse* (a horizontal line with an arrow pointing backward).

*Correct:* Pull the SLOW handle — this brakes the mechanism, reducing its speed to a near-crawl. The gears slow. This is required before setting the Clock Dial; the dial can't be precisely set on a running mechanism.

*Wrong:* Pull the REVERSE handle — the mechanism jolts backward. The sudden vibration shakes the vine clusters on the upper walls. **2 Void Bats** drop from cracks in the chamber ceiling and attack. The mechanism is now running in reverse and must be braked before proceeding. (A party member can correct this with a successful **Arcana DC 12** check while the bats are alive — or after they're killed.)

> **DM Note:** The scholar's journal says "never reverse." Players who read it carefully will know which handle to avoid. Let them figure it out — don't remind them. If they reverse it, the bats are a consequence, not a punishment.

---

**Step 3 — Clock Dial** *(South wall — requires rotating bridge to S)*

A large circular dial mounted on the chamber wall — a clock face with symbols instead of numbers. The symbols are worn. The first-light mark (a horizon line bisected by a single rising ray) is visible with a **DC 13 History or Arcana check.** Ithyrael's journal has a sketch of it — if the party has the journal and a player specifically references it, the check is not required.

The dial has a pointer. Currently, it's approximately thirty degrees past the first-light position — the cult moved it here.

*Correct:* Rotate the dial until the pointer aligns with the first-light symbol. The mechanism, currently running slow from the brake, shifts toward alignment.

*Wrong (wrong symbol):* If the party sets it to the wrong position, the mechanism runs toward that position. The light through the windows shifts — the eternal dawn flickers badly, moving toward midday. **2 Void Bats** emerge. The dial must be corrected before proceeding.

---

**Step 4 — Pendulum Catch** *(West wall — requires rotating bridge to W)*

A catch mechanism mounted at the west wall, positioned at the centerpoint of the pendulum's swing. At this point in the puzzle, the pendulum is swinging slowly (the brake has slowed it). The catch is a heavy locking arm — it must be engaged when the pendulum passes through center. Timing matters.

*Correct:* **DC 14 DEX check** to catch the pendulum at dead center, or **DC 12 INT check** to calculate the timing and engage it precisely. On a success, the pendulum locks. The mechanism settles into its new position. The gears stop.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The catch engages with a sound like something very heavy coming to rest — deep and final. The pendulum stops. The gears stop. The chains stop.
>
> For a moment, nothing.
>
> Then the light through the windows changes. It was the wrong color before. Now it isn't. The dawn coming in through the four windows is the same dawn that covers Irian — warm, even, the particular quality of first light that doesn't move. The chamber is the same light as the world outside it.
>
> A gear housing near the center of the mechanism shifts. Opens. The Seal of Waking rises from inside it, carried up on a small platform of interlocked gear teeth. Warm. Pale gold. The right color.

*Wrong (bad timing):* The catch engages off-center. The pendulum slams against the catch arm. Everyone in the chamber: **DC 15 DEX save.** Fail: 4d10 bludgeoning damage from the shockwave. The catch fails. The pendulum continues swinging — reattempt the check.

> **DM Note:** The puzzle is not locked to a strict round timer, but the longer the clock runs between step 1 and step 4, the more the light degrades. Use this aesthetically — describe the light getting worse each time they rotate the bridge or take extra time deliberating. If they trigger both wrong-move bat encounters, the fight in the middle of the puzzle creates real tension. Don't rush them, but let the environment communicate that this matters.

## Beat 8: The Fractured Echo

The moment someone takes the Seal, she arrives.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> Your hand closes around the Seal.
>
> Something changes in the room. Not the light — the light is right now. Something else. A pressure behind the eyes, brief and sharp, like a sound at the edge of hearing.
>
> She is between you and the arch. She wasn't there before — she didn't walk in, she is simply present. A figure, medium height, humanoid in shape but not in substance. Her form doesn't hold still. It flickers — present for a moment, absent for a moment, in a different position than it was a breath ago. Not moving. Relocating.
>
> Her face, when it's visible, is attentive. Interested. She looks at the Seal in your hand.
>
> The words arrive without a voice. You hear them, but not with your ears.
>
> *"Are you here to release me?"*

Let the party respond. She waits one full round — she's genuinely uncertain whether these are cult agents or something else. She reads surface thoughts passively (she has *Detect Thoughts* at will) and will know within moments whether they intend her harm.

**If they attack immediately:** She doesn't take offense. She retaliates without drama.

**If they respond:** She listens. She doesn't negotiate, doesn't offer anything — but she's curious. She's been bound for a long time. She finds them interesting. At the end of the conversation, she attacks anyway.

> **DM Note:** The Seal does not release her. The party collecting the seals weakens her binding — it was the Deathless court's consolidation plan that caused this, and the seals will need to be reset before the binding is restored. Taking the Seal made things slightly worse. She knows this. She might even say it, gently, before attacking.

See [encounter_floor8_fractured_echo.md](encounter_floor8_fractured_echo.md) for full stat block, tactical reference, and battle map.

---

### After the Fight

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> She's gone.
>
> The chamber is quiet. The mechanism is still. Through the four windows, the dawn of Irian is the same light it has always been — the first breath of morning, permanent and warm.
>
> The Seal of Waking is in your hand. Small. Heavy. The right temperature.
>
> Outside, somewhere, you can hear birds.

> **DM Note — Irian's Condition:** The mechanism has been reset to its correct position. The dawn anchor is functional again. The corruption in the tower will not worsen while the mechanism holds — but without the Seal reinforcing it, the anchor will slowly degrade over time. Irian is stable for now, but this is not a permanent fix. Varaen should know this when they return.

## Session 6 — DM Quick Reference

### Key Information the Party Should Have by End of Session

- [ ] The cult has been in this tower — floor 3 carvings confirmed it; floor 7 notes confirmed they left intentionally and plan to return
- [ ] The cult's goal was to get the clock running again — breaking the dawn anchor to make the Seal's lock fail
- [ ] The mechanism's correct function is explained: the clock holds Irian's eternal dawn in stasis, and the Seal was integral to that lock
- [ ] Chataschesma is partially aware — she sent a fragment of herself through the moment the clock briefly ran
- [ ] Her final message: the party consolidating the seals may itself be dangerous ("your hands, or ours — the result is the same")
- [ ] Irian is stable for now but the anchor will slowly degrade without the Seal — Varaen needs to know

### Hooks for Session 7

- The cult is still out there. They left expecting to return in a season. That season is nearly up.
- Two seals remain. If the cult has been seeding corruption near manifest zones, they have other targets already prepared.
- Chataschesma's final words deserve attention: what does it mean that the seals "in one place" could be dangerous? Does Varaen know this?
- Varaen offered to speak again when they returned. He had names from the handler's journal he recognized.

### Adjusting Difficulty

| Situation | Adjustment |
|-----------|------------|
| Party heavily depleted entering the Clock Chamber | Skip the wrong-move bat encounters — describe the correct choices landing cleanly. The boss fight is the climax; don't arrive there empty. |
| Party blazes through the puzzle with no tension | Add a time-pressure element: every two bridge rotations, the light degrades one step (bright → dim → dark in the chamber). Darkness in the chamber empowers the Fractured Echo — she gains +2 AC in darkness. |
| Fractured Echo too difficult | Reduce HP to 120. Keep all abilities — the tactics and spells are the threat, not the health pool. |
| Fractured Echo too easy | She uses Reality Fracture twice if she rolls two 5s or 6s. At half HP, she uses both remaining Dominate Person charges in consecutive turns. |

### NPC Summary

| NPC | Role | Notes |
|-----|------|-------|
| **Chataschesma (Fractured Echo)** | Boss — partial Daelkyr manifestation | Not the real thing. Present only because the clock briefly ran. Her curiosity is genuine; her threat is real. |
| **Ithyrael Vonaen** | Dead scholar | Her journal is a puzzle resource. The party may want to bring her body back to Shae Mordai — her family name will mean something to Varaen. |
