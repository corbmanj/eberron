# Encounter: Chataschesma, the Fractured Echo — Floor 8 Clock Chamber

---

### Encounter Summary

```
Difficulty:  Deadly
Party:       4 players, level 9

Monster Roster:
  1× Chataschesma, the Fractured Echo — CR 13 (estimated)

She does not move through space. She relocates within it. The party's main tactical challenge is
that she is never where she was a moment ago — melee fighters cannot hold her, and she will
always have a clear line to the most vulnerable target. Her key threat is Feeblemind: if she
identifies the party's primary caster early, she will attempt to shut them down permanently.
Hold Dominate Person for a melee character who has been most effective.
```

---

**Traits:**

**Phase Walk (Bonus Action).** She teleports up to 30 ft to an unoccupied space she can see. This is her only means of movement. It does not trigger opportunity attacks.

**Fractured Form.** She cannot be grappled, restrained, knocked prone, or affected by difficult terrain. She ignores physical obstructions smaller than 1 ft thick when using Phase Walk.

**Corrupting Presence.** Any creature that starts its turn within 10 ft of her must succeed on a DC 18 WIS save or take 11 (2d10) psychic damage.

**Inevitable Withdrawal.** When reduced to 0 HP, she phases out of the plane rather than dying — her form dissolves, she is expelled back through the crack in Irian's moment. She cannot be permanently killed in this state. She delivers one final telepathic message as she goes.

---

**Actions:**

**Multiattack.** She makes two Phase Strike attacks.

**Phase Strike.** *Melee Spell Attack:* +9 to hit, reach 5 ft. *Hit:* 17 (3d8 + 4) psychic damage. *(Spell-like — not counterspellable.)*

**Reality Fracture (Recharge 5–6).** She tears a line of reality in a 30 ft path, 5 ft wide. Each creature in the line: DC 18 DEX save or take 33 (6d10) psychic damage, half on success. The line appears as a brief, visible seam — the wrong side of something showing through for an instant. *(Spell-like — not counterspellable.)*

*Note: She uses Detect Thoughts passively throughout the encounter — she is always reading surface thoughts. This is flavor, not a mechanical action, unless a party member is actively trying to shield their mind (DC 18 CHA save to resist).*

---

**Tactics:**
- Round 1: She opens by asking if they're here to release her (roleplay beat — see session6.md Beat 8). If they attack immediately, she retaliates with Hypnotic Pattern to disrupt action economy.
- She uses Phase Walk every round to maintain distance from melee and position behind whoever she's targeting next. She is never within melee range unless she chose to be.
- Priority targets: she identifies the party's primary caster via Detect Thoughts and uses Feeblemind on them at the first good opportunity. She saves Dominate Person for whichever melee character has been most effective — turning a heavy hitter is more disruptive than knocking one out.
- Reality Fracture: she uses it when two or more party members are in a line. She is patient with the recharge — she doesn't force it at a bad angle.
- At 0 HP: her form becomes transparent, then absent. A final message arrives:
  > *"You carry what you don't understand. The seals will not hold when they are all in one place. Your hands, or ours — the result is the same."*

---

**Loot:** None. She was never truly here.

### Battle Map Description

> This map must be rendered in a strict top-down, directly overhead bird's-eye view. The camera is directly above looking straight down, as if the map were a floor plan. Not isometric. Not perspective. Not a three-quarter view. Directly overhead — the viewer is looking straight down at the ground.

**Map size:** 70×70 ft. The chamber is circular. The outer ring of walkable floor is approximately 8 ft wide. The remainder of the interior is occupied by the mechanism housing, the bridge, and the central gear platform.

**Ground surface:** Stone floor in the outer ring — pale Aerenal limestone, worn smooth. The central 50 ft area is the top surface of the mechanism housing: dark iron, grated and ridged. A narrow metal walkway (the Turning Bridge, 6 ft wide) extends from the north of the outer ring to a raised central platform, 15 ft in diameter, where the Seal housing sits.

**Terrain features:**
- **The Turning Bridge:** A metal walkway spanning the chamber on a north-south axis (in its starting position). It pivots at the center of the chamber and can be rotated to face E, S, or W. After the puzzle, it rests at whatever position the party left it — use this to create natural asymmetry in the combat space.
- **Gear housing walls:** Rising from the mechanism surface at irregular intervals — large curved sections of gear housing that act as partial cover. Three or four protrude 4–6 ft above the mechanism surface, creating natural chokepoints and cover positions between the outer ring and the central platform.
- **Four wall mechanisms:** Mounted at the N, E, S, and W walls — levers and dials set into stone housings. The Pendulum Catch at the west is the largest, with the pendulum arm descending from the upper housing and taking up a narrow corridor along that side. After the puzzle, the pendulum is stopped at dead center — it bisects the west approach to the central platform.
- **Four windows:** Cut into the dome wall at the cardinal points, each 6 ft wide and 8 ft tall. After the puzzle is solved correctly, the light through these windows is warm and perfect dawn — the brightest points in the chamber.

**Environment:** The mechanism housing absorbs light. The four window-beams create defined bright corridors across the floor; outside those corridors the space is dim light. The walls have tendril growth in the upper sections — dark organic streaks descending from the ceiling toward the mechanism housings, thinning out where the mechanism reset pushed the corruption back slightly. The central platform where the Seal rose from is the warmest point in the room, lit from below by the now-open housing.

> Style: painterly and detailed, similar to a professional tabletop RPG battle map. Rendered strictly from directly above — top-down, no perspective, no isometric angle.
