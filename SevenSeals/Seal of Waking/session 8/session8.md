# Session 8: What It Costs

## Seal of Waking — Act VIII: Return

*A session for 4 level-10 characters. Estimated runtime: 3.5–4 hours.*

---

## Session Overview

The party returns to Sharn, delivers the Seal of Waking to Gendrick, and learns that the path to the seventh seal runs through someone who does not give information freely. They find the shop. They make a deal they cannot undo. The consequences arrive before the session ends.

---

> **DM Note — Session 7 Recap:** The party left Irian, said goodbye to Varaen (who gave them a letter of passage from the Undying Court and a piece of restored tower stone), reunited with Captain Nor at the dock, and sailed back to Korranberg. Nor opened up about the squid encounter during a night watch conversation. They parted ways on the Korranberg docks.

### Session Flow

| Beat | Type | Est. Time | Description |
|------|------|-----------|-------------|
| 1 | Travel / Transition | 15 min | Lightning Rail: Korranberg to Sharn |
| 2 | Roleplay | 45–60 min | Gendrick debrief — deliver the seal, discuss what comes next |
| 3 | Roleplay / Intrigue | 60–75 min | The Tallow & Lens — a deal with an unusual shopkeeper |
| 4 | Roleplay / Consequence | 30–45 min | The delivery and its aftermath |

## Beat 1: Lightning Rail to Sharn

There is nothing remarkable about this leg of the journey. The Lightning Rail from Korranberg to Sharn takes roughly a day. The party has ridden it before. They have a compartment, meals in the dining car, the particular rhythm of a train that moves faster than anything pulled by horses should.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The Lightning Rail pulls out of Korranberg on schedule, the elemental hum building under the cars as it climbs to speed. The city falls away behind you. The Breland countryside opens up — flat, agricultural, the kind of landscape that goes by without asking anything of you.
>
> You've been traveling for almost two weeks. Sharn shows up on the horizon the way it always does: too tall, the towers catching the light before anything else, rising out of the plains like something that shouldn't exist but does.

> **DM Note:** Use this beat for any between-session housekeeping — spell preparations, item discussions, whatever the party wants to handle in transit. It's a soft opening, not a scene.

---

## Beat 2: Gendrick

Gendrick's tower is where it always is, in the middle levels of Sharn where the rent is neither cheap nor grand. The door has been mended. There are still ants.

He is home. He is always home.

---

### The Arrival

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The door opens before you knock. Gendrick is standing in the middle of his study in the same kind of robe he always wears, surrounded by the same organized chaos of open books and stacked correspondence. He looks at all of you, one at a time.
>
> Then he looks at Ozzy's hands.
>
> "You have it," he says. Not a question.

---

### Delivering the Seal

When Ozzy hands over the Seal of Waking — the pop-up book with the rising sun — Gendrick handles it with both hands and holds it for a long moment. He opens it once, carefully, and watches the paper sun rise. He closes it.

> **Gendrick:** "Six. Can you believe we are only 1 seal away from our quest's end?"

He sets it down on the table with the deliberateness of someone who doesn't want to put it down but knows he should.

He will want to hear everything: the tower, the corruption, the puzzle, the echo of Chataschesma. He asks good questions. He listens to the answers. When the party gets to Chataschesma's parting line — *"The seals will not hold if they're all in one place"* — he goes quiet.

> **Gendrick:** "She said that."

He is quiet for a moment longer than is comfortable.

> **Gendrick:** "She's trying to unsettle you. It's what she does — leave a hook in a wound and walk away. Don't give it more weight than it deserves."

> **DM Note:** Gendrick is not lying. He genuinely believes this. He is wrong, but he does not know he is wrong. Do not play him as suspicious or evasive here — he is exactly what he appears to be: an old creature who has been working this problem for centuries and is perhaps too confident in his read of it. The dramatic irony is that his reassurance is accurate in form and incorrect in substance. The warning *is* trying to unsettle them. It is also true.

---

### The Seventh Seal

Gendrick pours tea. He does not sit.

> **Gendrick:** "The seventh seal. The Plane of Madness. Xoriat."

He says the name the way people say the names of places they have thought about for a long time and never gone.

> **Gendrick:** "I've been trying to find a manifest zone for longer than you've been alive. Collectively. There are records — old ones, fragmented — that suggest one existed somewhere in southern Khorvaire, or in the Eldeen Reaches. But manifest zones to Xoriat don't behave the way other manifest zones do. They move. They appear and disappear. They're not anchored the way Irian's zone is in Shae Mordai."

He sits now.

> **Gendrick:** "I don't have a location. But I do have a lead. I have a name."

He slides a piece of paper across the table. On it, in his careful handwriting:

---

**The Tallow & Lens**
*Carving Street, Fallen District, Lower Sharn*

---

> **Gendrick:** "A contact in my network flagged this. The proprietor of that shop has been asking questions about Xoriat for years — the kind of questions that suggest they know something, not that they're looking for it. I don't know who they are. I don't know what they want. But if anyone in Sharn has information about an active manifest zone, it's them."
>
> He pauses.
>
> "The Fallen District is not a pleasant part of the city. Be careful about who and what you agree to."

> **DM Note:** Gendrick's warning is genuine but vague — he doesn't know the contact is Lords of Dust. He is passing along a lead from his network and trusting the party to navigate it. The warning "be careful about what you agree to" is good advice that the party will almost certainly not take seriously enough.

---

## Beat 3: The Tallow & Lens

### The Fallen District

The Fallen District occupies the lowest inhabited levels of Sharn — above the Cogs but below everything else, a neighborhood that was largely destroyed during the Last War and rebuilt cheaply by whoever needed somewhere to live. The streets are narrow and low-ceilinged, the buildings packed close. The magical lights that illuminate the upper levels don't reach this far down. People use torches here. Lanterns. Braziers.

It is not a dangerous place if you are paying attention. It is a dangerous place if you are not.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The elevator stops a level above Fallen — there isn't one that goes further down — and you take the stairs. The air changes on the way down. Warmer. Closer. The smell of cooking and coal and something sharp underneath, like old magic or old stone.
>
> Carving Street is a narrow lane between two buildings that lean slightly toward each other, as if they've been having a conversation for decades and haven't finished. The Tallow & Lens is about halfway down — you'd know it from the sign even if you didn't have the address. A carved lens leaning up against a single tallow candle, the paint faded but legible.
>
> The window display is dense: glass instruments, bound charts, three jars of something preserved in amber fluid, a model of a ship that looks like it has been to the bottom of the sea. The door is unlocked.

---

### The Shop

The interior is larger than it should be, given the exterior. Shelves from floor to ceiling, organized with more care than the Fallen District usually sees. A glass case near the counter holds a dozen items that look expensive and are not labeled.

The proprietor is already watching when the party enters.

---

### Curios for Sale

If the party browses, Davath names a price without being asked. None of these are labeled. Davath knows what everything is. Davath does not know where most of them came from, which may or may not be true.

| Item | Price | Description | Effect |
|------|-------|-------------|--------|
| **Warforged Memory Core** | 180 gp | A 3-inch sphere of glass and metal, warm to the touch. | Holding it and concentrating plays a looping 30-second memory: a battlefield, smoke, someone shouting a name, then silence. Always the same memory. Always ends before the name resolves. Davath says it came from a Cyre-based unit. |
| **Khyber Compass** | 220 gp | Brass casing, needle made from a sliver of Khyber dragonshard. | Points to the nearest manifest zone, not north. Currently angled slightly downward in the shop. Davath offers no explanation for that. |

The compass is reading it now. The party is unlikely to understand this yet, but if someone buys the compass and uses it in session 9 while investigating the Cogs, it will point straight down the moment they enter the right district. Let them figure out what that means.

---

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> A bell above the door clangs as you enter the shop. The person behind the counter is human - middle-aged, wearing a shopkeeper's apron over clothes that are too fine for this neighborhood. They have the posture of someone who is not surprised by anything, and the eyes of someone who has been expecting you specifically.
>
> "Adventurers," they say. Not a greeting. An observation. "You smell of the sea. And something else." They tilt their head. "Irian, I think. Interesting."
>
> They don't ask how you found them.

---

### The Proprietor

**The proprietor's name, if asked, is Davath.** This is not their real name. They are a rakshasa — a Lords of Dust operative who has run this shop for eleven years as a base of intelligence gathering. They are not the most powerful rakshasa in Sharn, but they are one of the most patient.

Davath knows the party is here because of Gendrick's network. They know more about the party than they let on. They are not hostile and have no interest in being hostile — the Lords of Dust are playing a long game, and a transaction is worth more to them than a fight.

> **DM Note:** The party should not learn that Davath is a rakshasa this session. A DC 20 Perception check notices that their hands are reversed — thumbs on the outside, fingers oriented wrong — though this could be rationalized as an unusual deformity. Rakshasas are immune to spells of 6th level and below, resistant to nonmagical damage, and can read minds at will (they do not reveal anything they learn). Let the party feel like something is slightly off without making it legible.
>
> **On Insight checks against Davath:** Davath is reading the party's minds throughout the conversation — they know exactly what the party suspects the moment they suspect it. This does not make Davath harder to read; it makes Davath's performance more precise. Use the following as a guide:
>
> - **DC 13:** Something about Davath's helpfulness is off. Not dishonest — they answer every question directly. But they're never surprised. Never caught off-guard. The conversation has the feeling of a script being followed, not a negotiation being had.
> - **DC 17:** The party member senses that whatever Davath wants from this transaction, it isn't the goodwill or coin. The deal is too convenient. Someone giving up a Xoriat manifest zone location for a simple delivery doesn't add up — unless the delivery is the point.
> - **DC 22:** A specific, cold clarity: Davath already knows what they're going to say. Not anticipates — *knows*. Their responses arrive a half-beat early. Their attention tracks toward whoever is about to speak before they speak. This is not a person reading the room. This is a person who has already read the room, finished, and is now performing having not read it.
> - **DC 27+:** The party member understands, without being able to say why, that Davath is not afraid of them. Not because Davath is confident — because the concept of being afraid of the party has not occurred to them as a meaningful possibility. Whatever Davath is, the party is not a threat to it.
>
> **What Davath does with this information:** If Davath senses the party is suspicious (which they always do, because mind-reading), they do not become evasive. They become *more* forthcoming — carefully chosen irrelevant truths offered as gestures of good faith. "I'll be honest with you: I don't know who commissioned the shop's expansion in 984. I've never asked." True, meaningless, and delivered like a confidence. A party that confronts Davath directly ("Are you hiding something?") receives: "Everyone in the Fallen District is hiding something. I'm hiding that I paid too much for that ship model in the window and I've regretted it for six years." They will not break. They have been doing this for eleven years.
>
> **If the party refuses the deal on the basis of suspicion:** Davath accepts this without any sign of disappointment. "I understand. This isn't the kind of arrangement everyone is comfortable with." They do not negotiate, threaten, or sweeten the offer. They let the party leave. They have already weighed this possibility. It is acceptable to them.

---

### The Deal

Davath gets to the point without being asked:

> **Davath:** "You want to find a Xoriat manifest zone. I know where one is. It's been stable-ish for about eight months, which in Xoriat terms is nearly permanent. I can give you coordinates, local landmarks, the works. Everything you need to find it."
>
> They reach under the counter and produce a small lacquered box, roughly the size of a book. The seal on it is black wax with no mark.
>
> "In exchange: you deliver this. To Councilor Merev Aldran. He has an office in the Tradefair District — you won't have trouble getting an appointment, he sees constituents on Zol and Wir. You give him the box. You do not open it. He is expecting it as payment for his recent work on the harbor licensing reform, and you leave. I would prefer he not know who his benefactors are."

They pause.

> **Davath:** "When the box has been delivered, you'll receive everything I've promised. Not before."

> **DM Note:** If the party asks what's in the box, Davath smiles and says: "Something he's been waiting for." If they press, Davath adds: "If I wanted you to know what was in it, I would have put it in a transparent box." The box cannot be opened by mundane means — it requires a DC 28 Thieves' Tools check or a spell of 7th level or higher to breach the seal. There are no magical emanations detectable from the outside (Davath has seen to this). The party has no way to know what they are carrying.
>
> **What's in the box:** A device of Lords of Dust make — a precision instrument that, when opened in an enclosed space, detonates with enough force to destroy a room and everyone in it. It is targeted specifically at Aldran, who has been investigating rumors of supernatural involvement in the Sharn dockworkers' labor disputes — which the Lords of Dust have been orchestrating as part of a separate, longer-running operation. Aldran found a thread. Davath is cutting it.

If the party agrees, Davath produces a second item: a sealed envelope.

> **Davath:** "The coordinates are in here. They'll become readable when the box reaches its destination. Not before."

The envelope is handed to them. The writing inside is currently invisible — it will appear when the box is delivered.

---

### If the Party Refuses

> **DM Note:** If the party refuses the deal outright, Davath is pleasant about it. They are not the only option, just the best one — and Davath knows this. "There are other ways to find a manifest zone. This is simply the fastest." They offer no other deal and make no threats. The party will need to find the Xoriat location through other means — a longer research path, perhaps through the Korranberg library or another Chamber contact. This is a valid outcome but it adds sessions to the arc. If the party refuses and you want to keep the pacing, have Gendrick have another, slower lead — but make clear it will take weeks.

## Beat 4: The Delivery and Its Aftermath

### Tradefair District

Councilor Merev Aldran's constituency office is in the Tradefair District — middle Sharn, respectable but not wealthy. The office is staffed by two clerks. Aldran himself sees visitors on a schedule. Getting an appointment for the same day is possible with a decent Persuasion check (DC 12); walking in unannounced and being seen immediately is DC 18.

> **DM Note:** The party can approach the delivery however they want. If they are discreet — convincing, low-profile, no lingering — the Watch has no reason to connect them to what comes next. If they are memorable — loud, unusual in appearance, noticed by the clerks in a way that stands out — the Watch will have descriptions when they come asking. Let them be themselves. Don't nudge them toward or away from subtlety.

---

### The Handoff

When the box is delivered — handed to Aldran, or left with his clerk for him if he is unavailable — the envelope in the party's possession becomes readable. The ink appears as if it was always there.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The writing comes up slowly, like ink bleeding through wet paper but in reverse — dark letters resolving out of nothing, line by line, until the page is full.
>
> It reads:
>
> *Eldeen Reaches. From Passage, travel southeast four days by road, then two days on foot east-northeast into the Towering Wood's edge. At the tree line you will find a clearing, roughly circular, with a Gatekeeper wardstone at its center — knee height, covered in old Dhakaani runes. The zone anchors to the wardstone's base. It becomes active in the three hours surrounding midnight: ninety minutes before, ninety minutes after. The resonance is subtle. You will know it by the cold.*
>
> *Come alone if you can. The zone is unstable under significant magical pressure.*

> **DM Note:** Give the party a moment to read it and feel like the deal paid off. They got what they needed. Then move on — the aftermath will come in its own time. The line "come alone if you can" is a calculated touch — it's designed to make them consider splitting the party at the ambush site. The line about magical pressure is designed to discourage heavy spellcasting before they arrive, so the ambush lands harder.

---

### The Aftermath

> **DM Note:** How quickly the news of the explosion reaches the party is up to you and their behavior. Some options:
>
> - **If they went back to Gendrick:** A messenger arrives at his tower within a few hours. There's been an explosion in the Tradefair District. A city council member is dead. Gendrick asks if they know anything about it. How they answer is a character moment.
>
> - **If they're in a tavern or inn:** A news crier comes through. The Watch is asking for witnesses.
>
> - **If they were memorable during the delivery:** A Watch officer comes to find them before they've gone anywhere. Polite but serious. They'd like to ask a few questions.

The explosion is real. Aldran is dead. The investigation will eventually reach a dead end — the box left no traceable origin, Davath's shop will be closed and empty within the day, and the Lords of Dust do not leave loose ends. The party is the one loose end, and Davath has already weighed the risk and found it acceptable.

The Watch cannot prove anything. But the party knows what they did.

---

> **DM Note — The Coordinates Are a Trap:** Davath never had a Xoriat manifest zone location. The coordinates on the envelope are a fabrication — a specific clearing in the Eldeen Reaches near the Towering Wood, chosen because it is remote, lightless at midnight, and has an old Gatekeeper wardstone that could plausibly be mistaken for a planar anchor point. The Lords of Dust needed the party out of Sharn while the ritual in the old Cannith forgeworks was completed. The ambush waiting at those coordinates was arranged weeks ago.
>
> The party does not know any of this yet. End the session here — let them believe they have what they came for.

---

> **Session End:**
>
> They have the location. They know the path to the seventh seal. They know, on some level, what it cost to get there.
>
> The Eldeen Reaches. Sixty miles east of the Towering Wood. Three hours around midnight.
>
> One seal left.
