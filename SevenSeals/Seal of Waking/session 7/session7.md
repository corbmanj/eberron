# Session 7: The Long Way Home

## Seal of Waking — Act VII: Departure

*A session for 4 level-10 characters. Estimated runtime: 3–3.5 hours.*

---

## Session Overview

A closing chapter for the Seal of Waking arc. The party returns to Shae Mordai, says goodbye to Varaen, finds Captain Nor still waiting at the dock, and takes the sea voyage back to Khorvaire. No combat required. The session is about decompression — letting the weight of six seals and a dead plane settle before the next arc begins.

> **DM Note — Session 6 Recap:** The party climbed the tower, fought two Tendril encounters (including Steven), solved the clock mechanism puzzle, and expelled Chataschesma's fractured echo. They retrieved the Seal of Waking — a pop-up book with a rising sun, currently held by Ozzy. The party leveled to 10. The tendrils receded as they descended. They are standing at the base of the tower with the option to explore Irian before returning to Shae Mordai.
>
> **What Chataschesma said:** She knew the party by name — Ozzy's in particular, suggesting minions have been actively reporting on them. Her parting line: *"You carry, but you do not understand. The seals will not hold if they're all in one place. Your hands are our own. The result is the same."* This is unresolved and should weigh on the session.

## Beat 1: Irian — The Morning After *(Optional)*

> **DM Note:** This beat only happens if the party chooses to explore Irian before returning to the manifest zone threshold. If they head straight back to Shae Mordai, skip to Beat 2. Either choice is fine — this is texture, not plot.

### What Happened While They Were in the Tower

When the party released the escapement and wound the mechanism forward, Irian experienced time moving for what may be the first time in its history. The plane of eternal dawn had morning. Then midday. Then afternoon. Then dusk. Then — for the first time in the memory of anything that lives here — night. Stars, if Irian has stars. Darkness, if it doesn't.

Then pre-dawn. Then dawn again, real and correct, the way the mechanism intended.

All of this happened while the party was inside the tower, fighting. By the time they came down, Irian had already returned to its proper state. But the inhabitants — the creatures, the constructs, the native things — went through all of it.

---

### Stepping Outside

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The tower behind you is white again. Not completely — the upper floors are still stained, the deep corruption doesn't just scrub out — but the color is returning. The stone is pale where it was black.
>
> The path down the hill is clear. The tendrils are gone. Not receding, not retreating — gone, like they were never here, except for the dark marks they left in the soil where they ran. The grass is very green in the places where they were.
>
> The dawn light is right. You didn't notice how wrong it had been until it wasn't anymore.

Let them take this in. Then:

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> There's a Dawnwarden sitting on the hillside.
>
> Not a broken one. Not flickering, not pulsing wrong. This one is intact — the light in it steady, warm. It's sitting the way something sits when it doesn't have a word for what just happened to it. Facing east. Perfectly still.
>
> It doesn't turn when you approach.

---

### The Dawnwarden

This is not a combat encounter. This Dawnwarden was far enough from the tower to avoid corruption. It has been a guardian of the outer reaches of this part of Irian, and it experienced the full passage of the day.

It communicates in impressions — not words, not sound. If someone approaches and gets close, they receive a sequence:

- *Light changing — moving — brightening past what it should be*
- *The particular sensation of midday, which this creature has no category for*
- *Dimming. Cooling. The color draining from everything.*
- *Darkness. Full darkness. The stars (just a handful — cold and unfamiliar)*
- *A long stillness*
- *Then the horizon brightening. The correct color. The right warmth*
- *Dawn*

The Dawnwarden isn't distressed. It isn't relieved either, exactly. It's sitting with something it doesn't have a concept for yet. The closest emotion it transmits is something like: *I didn't know that was possible.*

> **DM Note:** Let the party react to this however they want. There's nothing to solve here. The Dawnwarden will eventually stand, orient itself toward the tower, and resume its patrol — the plane has work to do now that the corruption is gone. If the party tries to communicate back, it receives them. If they try to explain what happened, it understands more than it should. It has been watching this plane for a very long time.

---

### The Garden

The path back to the manifest zone threshold passes through the lush central garden. It looks different.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The garden is the same garden. The path, the flowers, the colors that are too vivid.
>
> But the flowers are open in a way they weren't before. Not more open — differently open. The petals have a faint crease at the edge, the kind a thing has when it's been folded and then unfolded. These flowers closed last night. For the first time. And now they're open again, for the first time in the second sense.
>
> A bird lands on a branch near the path and looks at you. Just looks. Then it looks at the horizon. Then back at you.
>
> Then it sings — the same song it sings every morning, the song it has sung at every dawn since the plane was made, the song that has never meant anything except *light is here.* This morning it sounds like it means it.

---

### The Threshold

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The threshold back to Shae Mordai is where it always was — the air changes at the invisible line, the warmth shifting to something cooler and more ordinary.
>
> You look back once. The tower on the hill, white and still, the path clear and green. The Dawnwarden is a small shape of light on the hillside, moving again now, resuming whatever it was doing before the world changed.
>
> The Seal of Waking is in Ozzy's hands. Six seals. One to go.

## Beat 2: Return to Shae Mordai — Varaen

The party steps back through the manifest zone threshold into Shae Mordai. The garden is quiet. The deathless are where they always are. But something has changed in the quality of the air — there is a stillness here that wasn't here before, the stillness of a held breath finally let go.

Varaen is in the garden when the party steps through. He has been here most of the morning. He did not come to wait — he came because there was a child asking questions, and Varaen has never been able to walk past a child asking questions.

---

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The garden path is quiet. Near the center, where the flowers are densest, Varaen is crouched down beside a small elven girl — maybe six or seven, young enough that her ears are still growing into themselves. She's pointing at something in the dirt and he's looking at it with the same attention he'd give a diplomatic dispatch.
>
> He notices you before she does. He straightens slowly, the way very old people straighten, and says something to her in Elvish — short, gentle. She looks up. Stares at all of you with open curiosity. Then runs off down the path toward the city.
>
> Varaen watches her go, then looks at you.
>
> "She wanted to know why the flowers smelled different this morning." He pauses. "I didn't have an answer for her. I thought perhaps you might."

---

### What Varaen Felt

The deathless of Shae Mordai are attuned to Irian — they draw their sustained existence from the manifest zone, from the positive energy that bleeds through. When Irian experienced a full day, the deathless felt all of it.

They don't have words for some of it. The Undying Court has existed for thousands of years and in all that time the manifest zone has been a constant: the same warmth, the same quality of light, the same unchanging pressure of positive energy. Yesterday it changed. It dimmed. It went dark. Then it came back, and it was right in a way it hadn't been in years, maybe decades, maybe longer — the corruption is gone.

Varaen felt this. He sat with the deathless through it. He did not know if the party would come back out.

---

### The Conversation

Varaen does not ask for a full accounting. He can tell from looking at them that it was hard. What he wants is simple: to understand what they did, and to say thank you properly.

If the party tells him about the mechanism — the clock that held Irian frozen — he understands immediately. He is old enough to have heard the theory. He had not known if it was real.

If they tell him about Chataschesma appearing in the tower, his expression changes. Not fear. Recognition.

> **Varaen (quietly):** "She has been here before. Not in my memory. In the memory of the elders who came before me. There was a time when something like her moved through this place — it did not stay. It left marks."
>
> He pauses. "I did not know the marks on the tower came from something with a name."

He doesn't press for more. He is too old to press.

---

### The Goodbye

Varaen produces two things:

**A letter of passage** — written in High Aerenal, sealed with the sigil of the Undying Court. It identifies the party as guests who served Shae Mordai honorably. In most circumstances it is a formality. In the right circumstances, it could open a door.

> **DM Note:** The letter has no mechanical function right now. It's a prop and a future resource — if the party ever needs to invoke the goodwill of the Aerenal elves, this is how. File it away.

**A small piece of the tower stone** — white, smooth, the size of a thumb. Taken from the base of the tower after the corruption receded.

> **Varaen:** "The tower was white when it was built. It will be white again, in time. I thought you might want a piece of what you gave back to us."

> **DM Note:** Varaen presents this as just a stone. It is more than a stone — but its magic is quiet enough that he may not know, or may simply not say.

---

**Irian Clock Stone** *(magic item, requires attunement)*

The stone is always warm. In darkness it produces no light, but within 10 ft of it, dim light always seems slightly brighter than it should — as if a dawn is happening just out of sight.

**First Light.** Once per long rest, the attuned holder can touch a creature as an action and end one of the following conditions on it: blinded, charmed, frightened, paralyzed, poisoned, or stunned. The stone is from a place where corruption left. So can this.

---

He walks them to the edge of the garden. He does not follow them to the dock. He watches until they are out of sight.

## Beat 3: The Dock — Captain Nor

It has been ten or eleven days since the party left Nor at the dock. He said he'd wait two weeks. He is still here.

He has not been idle. The ship is in good repair. He has been catching fish and selling them at the Shae Cardel market to cover his docking fees. Reginald has been living her best life aboard, hunting insects along the dock pilings.

When the party arrives, Nor is sitting on a coil of rope mending a net. He looks up. He does not look surprised.

---

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The dock smells like salt and pitch and something faintly floral that probably blew in from the city. Captain Nor is sitting on the dock beside the ship — your ship, still here — mending a net with the automatic focus of someone who doesn't need to think about what their hands are doing.
>
> He looks up when he hears you. His expression doesn't change much, but something in his shoulders settles.
>
> "You're alive," he says. "Good. I caught a lot of fish. I'd rather not eat them alone."

---

### What's Changed

Nor has had ten days to sit in a foreign port with very little to do except think. He has been thinking about the squid. He has been thinking about what he saw come out of it — the tentacles of light, the energy that grabbed him. He has not told anyone what he saw because there is no one here to tell and he is not sure he has words for it yet.

He has also been thinking about Khorvaire. About the routes he knows, the ports he's worked, the life he has built. He is not sure it is the life he wants to keep building.

He hasn't made a decision yet. But he's close to one.

When the party is ready to leave, he offers without being asked:

> **Nor:** "I'll take you back. Same rate. I've been thinking I need to go anyway — there's nothing keeping me here, and everything I've been avoiding is somewhere west of here."

He says this lightly, like it isn't significant. It is significant.

---

> **DM Note:** Don't force the backstory. Nor will open up if the party engages with him, especially on the water. If they don't ask, that's fine — he ferries them back and is a pleasant presence. If they do ask, he has something real to say. See Beat 4.

## Beat 4: The Voyage Home

The voyage from Shae Cardel to Korranberg takes three to four days with good wind. The weather is clear. The sea is flat. There is nothing threatening in the water.

This beat is pure texture. There are no encounters planned. The goal is to give the party space to breathe, talk to each other, and exist in the world for a moment before the next push begins.

---

### The Ship

Nor runs a clean ship. The party has their quarters in the sleeper section aft. Meals are fish prepared multiple ways — Nor is quietly a good cook and mildly offended when this surprises people. Reginald patrols the deck with authority.

---

### The Dream

On the second night, everyone is below deck and asleep. Nor has the watch.

Some time around the middle of the night — the hour when the sea is quietest — Tam's scar begins to glow. Not brightly. Just enough that anyone sleeping near him would notice if they were awake. The scar has done this before, during bad dreams and after long rests, but tonight the pulse is slower and more deliberate.

The dream spreads. The rest of the party doesn't choose to enter it — they are simply there, the way people are simply in dreams. One moment they are asleep. The next they are standing on the deck of the ship.

---

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> You're on the ship. That much is right.
>
> Everything else is wrong. The sea is too still — not calm, but frozen, a surface that doesn't move at all, the color of deep water and something else at the same time. The sky has no stars. No moon. Just dark, flat, unbroken from one horizon to the other.
>
> The deck beneath you glows faintly at the seams, like something is lit from below. The rigging is there. The masts are there. The lantern at the bow is burning — but there's no flame, just light with no source.
>
> You look at each other. You are all here. You are all holding whatever you were holding when you fell asleep.
>
> Then you hear something from below deck. Several somethings. Moving toward the hatches.

---

See [encounter_dream_four_waves.md](encounter_dream_four_waves.md) for full stat blocks, wave structure, dream mechanics, tactical reference, and battle map.

---

> **DM Note:** Nor is not in the dream. He is on deck, awake, watching the horizon — he does not experience any of this. When the party wakes, nothing on the ship will look disturbed. The sea will be the ordinary sea again. He will not know anything happened.

---

### Nor at Night

On the second evening, if anyone is on deck during Nor's watch — *before* the dream, or if the session order works differently — he will talk.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The stars are out. There's no moon yet. Nor is at the helm, which barely needs attending in this calm — his hand rests on the wheel more out of habit than necessity. He's looking at the water.
>
> He doesn't look over when you come up. He just says: "Can't sleep either?"

He will talk about the squid if asked. He describes it haltingly — not because he lacks the words, but because he keeps second-guessing whether he is describing it accurately. He is a man who trusts what he can see and touch, and what he saw that day has not fit neatly into any category since.

> **Nor:** "The thing that came out of it — I've seen leviathans. I've been on a ship that got caught in a storm for six days and I thought we were going to die every one of them. This was different. I felt like — it's going to sound strange — I felt like I was being *read*. Like whatever that thing was, it was looking at me and finding things out."
>
> He is quiet for a moment.
>
> "I put down my orange. Don't know why I remember that. I was eating an orange and I just... put it down."

He is not looking for answers. He is telling someone what happened to him because he has been carrying it alone and the party was there.

If the party tells him what the squid was — what they know about it — he listens without interrupting. He will ask one question: *"Is there more of it?"*

> **DM Note:** There is more of it. The party may or may not know exactly how much more. Whatever they tell him, Nor takes it seriously. He doesn't panic. He adjusts.

---

### Waking Up

When the Dream Sorcerer is destroyed, the party wakes. They are in their bunks, or wherever they fell asleep. The ship is dark. The sea outside is moving normally.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The ceiling of the ship's quarters is above you. Wood. Real wood, with actual grain. Someone's breathing nearby. Outside, you can hear the water against the hull — the ordinary sound of a ship moving through an ordinary ocean.
>
> Tam's scar is not glowing.

They will have HP damage and spent spell slots carried over from the dream. They will not have completed a long rest — the dream interrupted that. If they go back to sleep, they can finish it (another 4 hours minimum), or they can choose to stay awake until Korranberg.

The dream is not discussed at breakfast. Or it is, very quietly. Nor notices that everyone looks tired in a specific way and asks no questions.

---

### Arriving in Korranberg

On the fourth morning, the familiar coastline of Khorvaire appears.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The Korranberg docks come into view as the sun clears the water. The smell changes — coal smoke and river mud, the particular density of a working port. After a week and a half on an island where nothing smelled like industry, it lands differently.
>
> Nor brings the ship in smooth. Dockworkers take the lines. Reginald does not want to leave the ship.
>
> Nor shakes your hands on the dock — all of you, one at a time, with the grip of someone who means it. He doesn't say much. He doesn't need to.

> **DM Note:** This is likely the last time the party sees Nor. Let it breathe. If a player wants to say something to him, give it space. He'll have something real to say back. He is not a named villain, not a source of plot — just a man who crossed paths with people doing something important and kept his word about it.
