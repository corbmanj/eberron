# Session 11: The Crevices of Dusk

## Vecna: Eve of Ruin — The Last Distraction

*A session for 4 level-10 characters. Estimated runtime: 1–1.5 hours. This session is the bridge between the homebrew campaign and* Vecna: Eve of Ruin *as written — it should move fast and hit hard. Once the party lands in the Sigil Sanctum, hand off directly to the module.*

---

## Session Overview

The ritual breaks. Gendrick doesn't come back with the room — he's pulled somewhere else, and the tear he leaves behind still shows him, in pieces, bound to a chair on the other side. If the party goes through, they find Vecna himself, and Gendrick buys them exactly three rounds before his own plan kills him.

This is not a fight the party wins by winning. It's a fight they survive, and what's on the other side of surviving it is the actual start of *Vecna: Eve of Ruin*.

---

> **DM Note — Recap:** The party fought their way down through the Cannith forgeworks and killed the cult's leader, whose notes named a place called the **Crevices of Dusk** — liminal spaces that run between universes. In the lowest chamber they found the ritual itself: **Kendri Nex**, a high-ranking mage of the Cult of the Severed Tongue, directing five nothics and six cult fanatics in a working meant to extract secrets from a captive and feed them toward Vecna's remaking ritual. The captive was Gendrick. The fanatics don't fight back — dropping any one of them below half HP is enough to break the ritual's structure. That's where this session picks up.

## Beat 1: The Ritual Breaks

The moment a fanatic drops below half their hit points, the ritual's structure fails. Play this the instant it happens, mid-combat if needed — don't wait for the fight to fully resolve.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The nearest fanatic drops. The chanting stops mid-word.
>
> The circle of light around Gendrick's chair flickers. Holds. Flickers again.
>
> Kendri Nex turns from his podium. He starts a word none of you recognize.
>
> He doesn't finish it.
>
> Gendrick's chair is empty. His cloak is on the floor where his shoulders were, one sleeve still holding the shape of an arm that isn't there anymore.
>
> Above the chair, the air splits. Not a doorway — a crack, about four feet tall, dark on both sides of the light coming through the middle of it.
>
> Through the crack, for half a second at a time: Gendrick, bound to a different chair, in a different room. Then the crack narrows to a line. Then it opens again.

Kendri Nex doesn't try to close it or claim it. He watches it for three full seconds — long enough for the party to notice him doing nothing else — then runs, out the side passage, robes catching on the doorframe. The remaining fanatics and nothics scatter with him or simply stop fighting; this is not a fight worth finishing anymore, for anyone.

> **DM Note:** Kendri Nex escapes. He's a loose thread — useful later if you want a recurring Cult of the Severed Tongue presence during the *Eve of Ruin* arc, but nothing here requires the party to catch him tonight.

## Beat 2: Through the Crack

The rift doesn't close on its own and doesn't pull the party through the way the ritual chamber's rift did in earlier plans — this one waits. Let the party look, ask questions, and decide.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> Up close, the crack is wider than it looked — wide enough to step through sideways. The dark on either side of the light isn't black. It's the absence of a color you don't have a name for.
>
> Each time it opens, you see more of the room on the other side. A chair. Cord with no knot in it. Gendrick's face, once, turned toward something you can't see yet.

If the party hesitates or debates, that's fine — there's no clock counting down here. If they try anything other than going through (attacking the rift, trying to seal it, calling for help), let it fail gently: the crack doesn't respond to damage, doesn't widen or shrink from outside effort, and doesn't lead anywhere else. Eventually they go through. When they do:

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> Stepping through doesn't feel like stepping through a doorway. There's no threshold, no line to cross. One moment you're looking at the crack. The next, you're standing in the room it was showing you, and the forgeworks is gone.

## Beat 3: The Crevices of Dusk

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The room is square, maybe fifty feet on a side. The floor under your boots is wood, not stone — wide boards, gapped at the seams, some worn smoother than others, like years of feet have crossed the same few paths. There's no torch, no window, no glowing crystal — but the room is lit evenly, from no direction you can point to.
>
> Off to one side, heavy red curtains hang floor to ceiling, gathered in folds thick enough to hide a person behind. Above, out of easy reach, a lattice of ropes and pulleys crosses the ceiling, a few sandbags hanging at uneven heights.
>
> On the wall opposite the curtain, a second doorway is traced in faint lines, a few feet from the real one. There's nothing behind it. It's just there, like someone drew a door and then didn't finish the room around it.
>
> In the center: a single wooden chair. Gendrick is bound to it with cord that has no knot anywhere in it. The chair throws two shadows, and they point in different directions.
>
> Standing over him: a figure in dark robes, ordinary height, ordinary build. His left hand ends at the wrist in something that isn't quite bone and isn't quite metal — it moves like fingers anyway. His right eye socket is empty, the scar tissue old. His other eye is just a normal brown, and it's the only part of him that looks at you when you come in.
>
> Gendrick sees you over the figure's shoulder. He smiles — actually smiles, tired but real.
>
> "Your power is great, Lord Vecna," he says, "but you underestimate the power of distraction."
>
> Then, louder, to you: **"Buy me some time!"**

> **DM Note — The Audience (Perception DC 20, or passive Perception 20):** Anyone who clears this sees past the gap in the curtain — rows of seats, as many as the room could hold, and people sitting in them, watching. When Vecna acts, or when Gendrick speaks, a few of them murmur: a low, unison "oh," the sound an audience makes when something on stage lands.
>
> No one who doesn't clear the check sees or hears any of this — not even if they're looking straight at the curtain when you describe it. Don't explain why if they ask. If a player who saw it tries to point it out to one who didn't, the second player still doesn't see it. Say so plainly and move on.
>
> This is garnish, not plot — no mechanical effect, nothing the party needs to act on. Let it sit there and be strange.

Combat begins immediately. See [encounter_crevices_vecna.md](encounter_crevices_vecna.md) for the full stat block, the three-round script, and guidance on handling whatever the party tries.

> **DM Note — What Vecna's actually after (yours only, not for the table):** Vecna is trying to pull the location of the seventh seal out of Gendrick before his rivals or the party can act on it. There is no seventh seal to find — Xoriat isn't a place you retrieve something from, it's where Azathoth sleeps, and going looking for a "seal" there is itself the danger. Gendrick knows this, or close enough to it. He does not have time to explain it here. Save the fuller version of this for later in the *Eve of Ruin* arc.

## Beat 4: Gendrick's Sacrifice

Run the three rounds from the encounter file. At the end of round 3, Gendrick's working completes and Vecna strikes him down. Narrate it plainly — this isn't a roll, it's a certainty.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> Gendrick's voice — you didn't notice him speaking until it stops mattering that he was quiet before — finishes on a word that isn't in any language you know.
>
> Vecna turns on him all at once. There's no windup. One moment Gendrick is finishing the word. The next, he isn't finishing anything.
>
> Vecna screams. Not Gendrick — Vecna. A sound with no language in it either, and for just a second the room shakes with it, hard enough that the two shadows under the chair swing toward each other and overlap.
>
> Light comes off the chair in every direction at once — not an explosion, more like something that was folded very tightly coming undone all at once.

> **DM Note — If anyone made the Perception check in Beat 3:** Under the noise, they hear it — the audience, applauding. Not a roar. Polite, sustained, the kind of applause a theater gives a performance it respected. It's the last thing that character hears before the light takes over. Don't tell the rest of the table why that player looks the way they do.

### Vecna's Link

As the light hits the party, something else happens at the same moment — small enough to miss in the noise, and worth calling out separately once things settle.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> It doesn't hurt. That's the wrong word for it.
>
> On the inside of your left wrist, a mark appears. No bigger than a thumbprint. The color of a bruise that hasn't finished forming. It's cold, and it stays cold.

> **DM Note — Vecna's Link:** The mark is permanent and magical. Dispel Magic does not remove it; Remove Curse (6th level or higher) suppresses it for 24 hours before it returns. It has two effects:
>
> 1. **Presence.** Vecna knows where each bearer is, to within a mile. He isn't watching constantly — he has larger concerns now — but he knows they exist and roughly where they are. The mark occasionally pulses cold when something nearby is relevant to his attention.
>
> 2. **Pull.** Bearers have a faint, wordless sense of the direction of Vecna's primary location. This is a narrative tool, not a compass — a vague wrongness in a particular direction. It becomes mechanically relevant as the *Eve of Ruin* arc continues.
>
> The party will want to remove it. Tell them plainly that they can't, not yet, if they try.

## Beat 5: The Sigil Sanctum

The force damage from the backlash (see the encounter file's Round 3) is the same energy that throws the party out of the Crevices of Dusk. There's no transition to narrate slowly here — cut hard.

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The light doesn't fade. It just becomes a different light.
>
> You're standing on a floor that isn't the Crevices' wooden boards anymore — polished, dark stone, inlaid with lines of silver that are actually moving, slowly, like something is still being drawn. The ceiling is higher than it has any business being. Three figures stand around a circle of the same silver lines, mid-gesture, and all three of them just turned to look at you.
>
> One is a woman with a sword across her back, mouth open on the start of a word she didn't get to finish. One is a halfling in traveling clothes, hand still raised toward the circle. The third — tall, robed, a Mordenkainen you don't recognize the details of quite right — is staring at you with an expression that isn't confusion. It's closer to being caught.

> **DM Note — Handoff:** This is the Sigil Sanctum, mid–Wish spell, with Kas wearing Mordenkainen's face. From here, hand off directly to *Vecna: Eve of Ruin*'s Sigil Sanctum sequence in the book. The party's arrival interrupts the casting — use whatever consequence the module specifies for an interrupted Wish, or improvise a minor one if you'd rather not derail the scene mechanically. Don't resolve anything else about this room tonight; end the session here.

---

> **Session End:**
>
> Gendrick is dead. He knew he would be before he said a word to you.
>
> The seals are gone again — scattered on purpose, this time, by the one person who understood what they actually were.
>
> Something on your wrist knows where you are.
>
> And you're not in Sharn anymore.
