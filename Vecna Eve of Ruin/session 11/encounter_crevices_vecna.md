# Encounter: The Crevices of Dusk

---

### Encounter Summary

```
Difficulty:  Unwinnable (scripted survival — 3 rounds)
Party:       4 players, level 10

Monster Roster:
  1× Vecna — CR 30 (narrative ceiling, not a target)

This is not a fight the party can win by dealing damage. Vecna regenerates
fully every round (see Traits) and cannot be reduced, banished, or otherwise
removed from the scene. The win condition is surviving three rounds while
Gendrick completes his spell. Vecna's actions are scripted round by round
below — run them regardless of what the party does. The party's actions are
NOT scripted — let them try anything. Adjudicate using the guidance under
Tactics.
```

> **DM Note — Why this works:** The party cannot stop this encounter from ending the way it ends. Gendrick dies. The seals scatter. That outcome is fixed. What's NOT fixed is how the party spends their three rounds getting there — let them be heroic, reckless, clever, or scared. All of it is fine. All of it leads to the same door.

**Traits:**

**Undying.** At the start of each of Vecna's turns, he regains all lost hit points. This is his domain — within the Crevices of Dusk, he cannot be reduced, only delayed.

**Legendary Resistance (3/Day).** If Vecna fails a saving throw, he can choose to succeed instead.

**Missing Pieces.** Vecna's left hand ends at the wrist in something that isn't quite bone and isn't quite metal — it moves like fingers anyway. His right eye socket is empty, rimmed with old scar tissue; his remaining eye is an ordinary deep brown. Neither absence slows him down.

---

**Actions:**

**Withering Touch.** *Melee Spell Attack:* +18 to hit, reach 10 ft. *Hit:* 36 (8d8) necrotic damage, and the target's hit point maximum is reduced by that amount until the target finishes a long rest. *(Spell-like — not counterspellable.)*

**The Severing Eye.** Vecna turns his remaining eye on up to six creatures he can see within 60 ft. Each target must make a DC 20 Wisdom saving throw, taking 33 (6d10) psychic damage on a failure, or half as much on a success. Regardless of the save, a cold mark appears on the inside of each target's wrist (see **Vecna's Link**, in the session document). *(Spell-like — not counterspellable.)*

**Tactics — The Three Rounds:**

Run these regardless of what the party does. Vecna does not deviate from this script — Gendrick's spell is already unfolding, and Vecna is racing to finish his interrogation before it completes, not managing the party as a threat.

- **Round 1 — Testing.** Vecna doesn't consider the party a threat yet. He continues addressing Gendrick. If anyone approaches within reach, he strikes them with **Withering Touch** without turning his head — a warning, not an opening move. If no one approaches, he uses Withering Touch on whoever is nearest to Gendrick.

- **Round 2 — Irritation.** Gendrick still hasn't answered him. Vecna uses **The Severing Eye** on the whole party at once. This is the round every PC receives the wrist mark, regardless of position or action taken. Read the mark's description in full here (see session document) — it should land as a genuine event, not a side effect.

- **Round 3 — The Spell Completes.** Gendrick's working finishes. Vecna feels the seals leave the Crevices and turns on him fully — this is the killing blow, and it is not aimed at the party. Describe Vecna striking Gendrick down (no roll needed; this is scripted). The backlash from three forces meeting at once — Gendrick's spell, Vecna's strike, and Gendrick's death — floods the chamber. Every creature present (the party) must make a DC 18 Constitution saving throw, taking 22 (4d10) force damage on a failure or half as much on a success. This is also the moment the party is expelled from the scene (see session document).

**Handling party actions in between:**
- **Attacking Vecna:** Let them roll. Damage lands, wounds visibly open — and close again at the top of his next turn (Undying). Describe it plainly: the cut closes like it was never there.
- **Trying to free or move Gendrick:** He's anchored to his own working — moving him ends the spell early and hands Vecna the win outright. If someone tries, Gendrick refuses with whatever breath he has: "No — let me finish this."
- **Trying to banish, restrain, or otherwise remove Vecna:** Legendary Resistance covers it. He simply doesn't fail the save.
- **Retreating, hiding, or otherwise not engaging:** Completely valid. "Survive" doesn't require heroics. Vecna's scripted actions still land on whoever's nearest to him or to Gendrick.
- **Something genuinely clever that doesn't fit the categories above:** Reward the idea narratively — let it change how the scene reads — without changing the outcome. Gendrick still dies at the end of round 3. That part isn't negotiable, and the players shouldn't be able to tell it was ever in doubt.

---

**Loot:** None. This is not a fight where Vecna can be defeated, and nothing here is meant to be looted — the point is what the party carries out, not what they pick up.

## Battle Map Description

> This map must be rendered in a strict top-down, directly overhead bird's-eye view. The camera is directly above looking straight down, as if the map were a floor plan. Not isometric. Not perspective. Not a three-quarter view. Directly overhead.

**Map size:** Roughly 50×50 ft, though the space does not hold its own edges consistently — render it as a defined square room for the purposes of the map, with the irregularities described below as surface detail rather than shifting geometry.

**Terrain:**

- **The chair (center):** A single wooden chair sits at the exact center of the room, facing left toward the curtain wall — its back to the right wall. Gendrick is bound to it with cord that has no visible knot. The chair casts two shadows, pointing in different directions.

- **Floor:** Wide wooden boards, not stone — a stage floor. The boards are gapped at the seams and not quite uniform in width; some patches are worn smoother than others, as if the same paths across the room have been walked for a very long time.

- **Curtain (left wall only):** A single heavy red curtain hangs from a track running the full length of the left wall, currently drawn fully open. Pulled back, the fabric gathers into two small bunched clusters — one tucked into the top-left corner, one into the bottom-left corner — each covering only about the top 5% and bottom 5% of that wall's length. The remaining ~90% of the left wall, the whole middle stretch between the two clusters, has no curtain fabric at all — just the open edge of the room. Do not place any curtain fabric along the top, bottom, or right walls.

- **Walls:** The top, bottom, and right walls are bare, except for a second, fainter doorway drawn in outline near the real one on the right wall — a door that isn't there, rendered as a faint architectural line for texture only.

- **Rigging (ceiling motif, for reference only):** A lattice of ropes and pulleys crosses the space above, with a few sandbags hanging at uneven heights — render as a faint overhead texture, not a usable terrain feature.

- **Ceiling/lighting motif:** No visible light source. The room is lit anyway, evenly, from no discernible direction — render as flat, shadowless overhead lighting with the two-shadow detail on the chair as the one exception.

- **No cover, no elevation, no hazards beyond the encounter itself.** This is an empty stage built around one chair. The emptiness is the point. Do not render any audience, seating, or figures beyond the chair — those exist only in the session's narration, never in the map.

> Style: painterly and detailed, similar to a professional tabletop RPG battle map, but with a faint wrongness underneath — board seams slightly askew, one extra doorway outline that leads nowhere, a stage that shouldn't be here. The red curtain hangs on the left wall only, drawn open and bunched into small clusters at just the top-left and bottom-left corners (~5% of that wall's length each) — the middle of the left wall, and the top, bottom, and right walls, show no curtain fabric at all. Rendered strictly from directly above — top-down, no perspective, no isometric angle.
