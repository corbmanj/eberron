# Session 12: The Wizards Three

## Vecna: Eve of Ruin — Chapter 2: Sigil Sanctum

*A session for 4 level-11 characters. This session is a hinge point — Beat 1 is homebrew (bridging session 11 into the book), Beat 2 hands off directly to the printed adventure.*

---

## Session Overview

The party lands hard, in the middle of someone else's spellcasting, ninety years and an unknown number of planes away from the last place they stood. Three archmages are the first thing they see. One of them is not what he appears to be, and nobody in the room — including the party — is going to find that out today.

This session is short by design. Beat 1 is the whole homebrew portion: the arrival, the wizards, the pitch, the item, the rest. Beat 2 opens with one added area (W0, a choice point before the book's dungeon proper even starts) and then hands off straight to Web's Edge as printed — from there the DM has the wheel.

---

> **DM Note — Session 11 Recap:** The party survived three rounds against Vecna in the Crevices of Dusk while Gendrick completed his working. Gendrick scattered the six seals across the multiverse and died for it. Each PC carries Vecna's Link — a cold, permanent mark on the inner wrist. The backlash threw the party through, mid-light, into wherever they are now.

## Beat 1: The Wizards Three

> **Read Aloud:**
>
> The light doesn't fade. It just becomes a different light.
>
> You're standing on polished dark stone, inlaid with lines of silver that are actually moving, slowly, like something is still being drawn. The ceiling is high — higher than it has any business being — and hung with globes of steady white light that don't flicker at all. The air smells like hot metal and old paper.
>
> Three figures stand around a circle of the same silver lines, mid-gesture, hands still raised. All three of them just turned to look at you.
>
> One is a tall woman, silver hair loose down her back, a plain traveling cloak over robes that look like they cost more than they look like they cost. Her hands are shaking — not with fear, with effort, the way hands shake after holding something heavy for too long.
>
> One is shorter, younger-looking, dark hair escaping a braid in every direction, ink stains up both forearms. She's the one who sits down first — drops straight onto the floor, cross-legged, and starts laughing, quietly, at nothing anyone said.
>
> The third is tall, grey-bearded, robed, leaning on a black staff capped in silver. He doesn't sit. He looks at you the way someone looks at a math problem that just solved itself.
>
> Nobody says anything for a second. Then the silver-haired woman speaks.
>
> "Well," she says. "That's not what we were casting for. But I suppose it will do."

---

### The Wizards

**Alustriel Silverhand.** Silver hair, plain traveling cloak, exhausted hands. Ruled the city of Silverymoon for the better part of a century and a half before stepping back from the throne; one of the Seven Sisters, a Chosen of the goddess of magic. Warm, direct, visibly running on fumes. She does most of the talking that isn't Kas.

**Tasha.** Dark hair, ink-stained forearms, sitting on the floor. Also goes by Iggwilv in older company — adopted daughter of Baba Yaga, author of a book on demons she'd rather not discuss in front of new people, one of the most dangerous archmages alive dressed like she just came in from a field survey. Treats near-apocalypse with the same energy she'd treat a mildly annoying rainstorm. This is not indifference. It's how she stays functional.

**"Mordenkainen."** Grey beard, black staff, the driest voice in the room. Everyone here — every NPC, every PC — reads him as exactly who he appears to be: one of the most storied archmages in the multiverse. He is not. He is Kas, wearing a Crown of Lies that makes the deception total. Play him precise, a little imperious, utterly unbothered. Nothing in his manner should tip the party off — that reveal is not for this session.

> **DM Note — If Ozzy is present:** Ozzy has been hearing from "Mordenkainen" for a while now, through his crown. This is the first time he's seen the man in person. When "Mordenkainen" first speaks, have Ozzy notice something — the cadence, the low dry register, something under it he's heard before, privately. He doesn't say anything yet. He's just watching this man very closely now. Let this sit as a private thread for Ozzy's player, not a table-wide announcement — the same way the Crevices' audience was a private detail for whoever spotted it.

### The Explanation

Alustriel does most of the talking, with Tasha interjecting and Kas correcting details with clinical precision.

> **Alustriel:** "You'll want the short version, and I don't have the strength for a long one. Vecna intends to remake everything — every plane, every world, every soul in it — with himself at the center. Not ruling the multiverse. Being it. The three of us have spent longer than I'd like to admit trying to find a way to stop that from happening. Tonight we tried a wish. It didn't do what we asked it to do."
>
> **Tasha** *(from the floor):* "It did something, though. It brought you four here instead. That's not nothing. Wishes are picky about what counts as 'something.'"

The wish's failure — engineered by Kas without any of the other two knowing it — is what pulled the party through. None of the wizards understand why yet, and Kas is not volunteering an explanation.

> **DM Note — Time, Briefly.** Alustriel can tell the party plainly: it has been nearly ninety years since a dragon named Gendrick gave his life to scatter the seals of a working meant to unmake the world. If the party reacts, let them — one beat, no more. Don't dwell here; the mission pitch should follow within a minute of table time. The weight of this is real and it's not going away, but tonight is not the night to sit in it. It'll keep.

If the party says some version of *"we were there"* — because they were — Kas has an answer ready, delivered without missing a beat:

> **"Mordenkainen":** "Then you'll have been carried here by the Fate Spinners. They do that — reach into a moment and pull the people who matter to it forward, when the moment isn't finished. I don't pretend to understand them. I don't think anyone does. But it explains why you're standing in front of me and not decomposing in a Sharn cellar."

He says it like a settled fact. It is not. Kas genuinely doesn't know why the party is here or how they've stayed young — he's improvising a plausible-sounding answer because the alternative is admitting he doesn't know, and Kas does not admit that.

> **DM Note — If a wizard remarks on the party not having aged:** The book doesn't cover this, so it's yours to play however feels right tonight. Alustriel is centuries old and sharp even exhausted — she might file it away with a look and nothing more, or she might ask outright. Neither answer is wrong. Nothing needs to be decided about it now.

---

### The Pitch

Kas is the one who turns the conversation toward a plan — suspiciously fast, though nobody in the room finds that suspicious tonight.

> **"Mordenkainen":** "Gendrick — Golden Dragon, Order of some renown, dead these eighty-nine years — didn't destroy what he was carrying. He scattered it. Seven pieces of a rod that, whole, is one of the few things in existence that could stop what Vecna intends. I don't know where the pieces are. I don't have that kind of sight. But the sanctum's portal does, or near enough — it's fused with an old Well of Many Worlds, and it can carry a bearer of one of these artifacts to exactly where they need to go. Find the pieces. Assemble the rod. That is, as far as the three of us can determine, the only plan left on the table."

> **Alustriel:** "Take this seriously. We don't know what's guarding any of these pieces, and we have no way to warn you before you arrive. Wherever the portal sends you, assume it's dangerous until you have a very good reason to assume otherwise."

**The first piece:** somewhere in the Underdark beneath Faerûn, in a place called Web's Edge. Once the party finds it, the piece itself will point the way to the next one.

### The Item

> **"Mordenkainen":** "I won't tell you Vecna can be beaten outright — he can't, not by four mortals with swords, not by three archmages who just emptied themselves into a wish that didn't work. But he can be delayed, and delay is the only currency that's mattered since the day Gendrick spent his life buying you some. Take this. If the day comes that he's weakened enough, use it."

He offers a small silver chime, engraved with fine sigils.

> **Chime of Exile** *(very rare, wondrous item)*
> While holding the chime, you can use an action to cast *Banishment* (spell save DC 20). If the target has 50 hit points or fewer, it automatically fails the saving throw. Once used, the chime can't be used again until the next dawn.

> **DM Note — Do not reveal to players:** Banishing Vecna with this item doesn't erase him — it sends him back to Oerth, his plane of origin. That consequence belongs to the endgame of this campaign, not to a session-12 item description. Give the party the mechanical text above and nothing more.

---

### Before They Go

The sanctum sits under a permanent *nondetection* effect — Alustriel's original reason for building it here in the first place, long before any of this. The party can rest here as long as they want before stepping through the portal to Web's Edge.

> **DM Note — Vecna's Link, Optional:** If you want a small mechanical payoff for resting here, the nondetection field is a reasonable reason for the wrist-mark to go quiet — no cold pulse, no sense of direction, for as long as the party stays in the sanctum. It picks back up once they leave. This is a nice detail if it fits the moment; skip it if you'd rather the mark stay a background constant. Either way is fine.

Tasha, Alustriel, and "Mordenkainen" all need real recovery time after the wish — expect them to be present but drained for any extended rest, unable to offer much beyond what's already been said. This is the party's last quiet moment before Web's Edge.

### Web's Edge Adaptation

#### W0: Approach Tunnel

*This area doesn't exist in the book — it's the party's point of entry, added so they arrive with a choice in front of them instead of standing at W1's door with nothing to do but open it. Keyed as W0 so it sits with the rest of the dungeon's numbering.*

> A rough stone tunnel, barely wide enough for the four of you to stand shoulder to shoulder in the side alcove where you've stopped. Water drips somewhere close by, out of sight. Down the tunnel, a door creaks open, then shut. A voice mutters a string of words that isn't any language you know, low and quick.
>
> Boots start moving. Four sets, unhurried, coming your way, carrying a torch between them.

This is the party's first look at Web's Edge, though they don't know it yet. The door and the incantation belong to a spellcaster inside area W1, sealing one of the two working doors behind a departing patrol (see "W1's Three Doors," below). Four **Devotees of the Spider Queen** *(see* [encounter_websedge_approach.md](encounter_websedge_approach.md) *for the full stat block)* are walking this tunnel outward, carrying rope and empty sacks, on their way to relay word of a planned assault to Lolth's agents embedded in a nearby mining operation (see area W7). They haven't noticed the party and have no reason to expect anyone this deep in the tunnel.

The characters have a genuine choice here, and the choice matters more than any fight would.

**Letting Them Pass.** If the characters stay hidden — a DC 13 group Stealth check is enough, since the devotees aren't looking for anyone — the four walk by, still talking, and continue down the tunnel until the dark takes them. They don't return this session. The characters lose nothing by this except four sets of robes and whatever the devotees knew.

**Taking Them by Surprise.** If the characters ambush the devotees from hiding, the devotees are surprised and act last. This fight is not a threat to the party — the only real decision is whether to leave one alive. A devotee who's questioned, even roughly, is happy to talk: they don't know much, but they know the middle of Web's Edge's three sealed doors is a dead end — nobody's ever gotten it open, and nobody's bothered trying in years — and they know the passphrase any Lolth devotee is expected to give at the door, "The Spider Queen smiles on you," because they've said it themselves a hundred times.

**Killing Them Without a Thought for What They're Wearing.** The characters may simply kill the devotees and move on without searching the bodies. Don't prompt them to loot. If nobody thinks to take the robes, the option to impersonate worshipers later is simply gone — that's a natural consequence, not a punishment.

**Loot.** Whether taken from bodies or simply found, the four devotees are carrying robes and spider-shaped talismans — together, enough disguise material for four people to pass as devotees of the Spider Queen (see "Impersonating Worshipers," Web's Edge). Also a coil of rope, two empty sacks, and a few silver pieces each.

**Development.** A fight here doesn't alert the interior of Web's Edge — the tunnel bends, and the rock is thick between here and area W1. A very loud or very long fight (more than a few rounds, or a thunderous spell) is fair game for the DM to let carry inward, at your discretion.

#### W1's Three Doors

The book prints this as two disconnected pieces: **W1 (False Front)** describes its northeast, east, and southeast doors as "rusted shut," while a separate general rule elsewhere states that the doors to **W3**, **W4**, and **W5** are sealed with an *Arcane Lock* (DC 20 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) with thieves' tools to open; *Knock* or *Dispel Magic* also work, the latter with no check needed). Nothing in W2 through W12 ever references a route back to W1, and W2 (the shrine) is a dead-end alcove, not a through-passage — so as written, the party has no printed way to actually get deeper into Web's Edge. Ruling for this table:

- **Northeast door:** Arcane Locked. Opens into **W3** (Summoning Chamber).
- **Middle / East door:** Genuinely, permanently rusted shut — no lock, nothing magical. If forced open (or if a character investigates first), it opens onto solid stone. A real dead end, exactly as the "false front" sells it.
- **Southeast door:** Arcane Locked. Opens into **W5** (Guardian Chamber).

The general rule's third Arcane Locked door — "the door to W4" — isn't one of these three. The party will hit it later, deeper in the complex, most naturally as one of the two iron doors that bookend W4's own corridor (see W4's text). Nothing from the general rule goes unused; it's just not front-loaded at the threshold.

This gives the party a real choice of entry point (into the summoning chamber or the guardian chamber) rather than one correct door, and keeps the shrine in W2 as a side room worth visiting rather than a required corridor.
